## Key Question 1: What abilities does each character have, and how are puzzles built so that both are needed (not one player doing everything)?

### Takeaway
The games that work best give each character a verb the other lacks *and* a body the other can use: a platform, an anchor, a rope, a distraction. The best puzzles chain those verbs so that A's action creates the space for B's. The common failure is "parallel" design: each player clears their own obstacle in turn ("you do your thing, I'll do mine"). Reviewers of Little Nightmares III and Unravel Two said exactly this.

### Cited Findings

**The Lost Vikings (1992). Three fixed, sharply separated abilities**
- Erik "can bash through some walls (and enemies) with his helmet" and runs faster. Baleog kills enemies with sword or bow, and his arrows also hit switches. Olaf blocks with his shield, glides with it, and can hold it up as a platform for the others — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Vikings)
- Blizzard's retrospective says Erik is the only one who can jump, and that Olaf's shield is a platform the other two stand on for extra height — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)
- Design rule: "the stages are built so that you can't cross them without using your Vikings to support each other." Every level needs all three Vikings at the exit — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Vikings)
- Example chain from Blizzard: Baleog opens a path, Erik breaks a wall and triggers an elevator, and Olaf's shield keeps him safe while he glides. Each character's action unlocks the next character's route — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)
- Shared inventory: keys, bombs and food can be swapped between Vikings "as long as they are close to each other." Handing an item over is a deliberate meeting point — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Vikings)
- The design started as Lemmings-like hundreds of Vikings and shrank "to fewer Vikings, down to five, and then three, each with very specific abilities" (Frank Pearce). Anhalt said this concentrated "the individual coolness in fewer units" — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)
- Levels keep most of the action in a small area, so once the puzzle is solved the Vikings can reach the exit quickly — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)

**Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons (2013). Older and strong vs younger and small, one stick each**
- Each brother has his own thumbstick, and each trigger makes that brother interact. The older brother is stronger: he pulls levers and lifts his brother up. The younger brother squeezes between narrow bars. Puzzles often have one brother distract a hostile character while the other gets past — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers:_A_Tale_of_Two_Sons)
- Fares: "you connect with your left hand to the bigger brother and right hand to the little brother." The puzzles were tuned to that control scheme: "A good example, for instance, is with the rope sequence or when you carry a log." — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/81361/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-not-meant-for-co); [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons)
- Reviewers' standout example: the brothers anchor one another while swinging on ropes to climb a castle. One holds fast while the other swings, then they swap — search excerpt from reviews listed at [GamerRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/61150-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-review) / [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-review) (exact outlet for this line not verified)
- Personality is written into the world interactions, not only into the puzzles. Each brother reacts differently to the same object. Fares's example is a flower: "if you push with the little brother he will toss it down and break it." A matching flower at the end lets the player "smell the flower as his big brother did" — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons)

**Unravel Two (2018). Two identical characters joined by a tether**
- The two Yarnys share one moveset. They differ in position, not ability, and the yarn between them is the puzzle tool. Sahlin: "That yarn between you kind of becomes your lifeline… The other character is essentially your anchor." — [TheSixthAxis hands-on with Martin Sahlin](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=316595)
- Concrete tether verbs: wrap the yarn around objects so the partner can climb up to you. Tie two grapple points to make "bridges and trampolines." Lasso points to swing. Use the partner as "an anchor point on ledges" to swing from any raised platform. A new wall jump was added — [GamingTrend review](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/you-and-me-till-the-end-unravel-two-review/)
- Examples from previews: leapfrogging, one Yarny as a swing point for momentum, one pushing an object so the other reaches higher ground, a yarn bridge pulled down to launch a Yarny higher, a box shifted back and forth to reach a split "spark" — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=316595)
- A review excerpt: "lasso the stick and pull it upright to make a handy wall jump for your co-op partner." One player changes the level so the other can move through it — search excerpt among Unravel Two reviews ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/review-unravel-two/), [Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/unravel-2-review), [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/unravel-two-review); outlet not verified)
- Criticism: "when 70% of the puzzles are solvable with a single Yarny, I begin to wonder why multiplayer is even present" — search excerpt, apparently from [Destructoid's review "It takes two, baby! (But not really)"](https://www.destructoid.com/review-unravel-two/) (attribution not verified by fetch)

**It Takes Two (2021) and Split Fiction (2025). A new paired ability set in every level**
- Opening level: "Cody gets a set of nails that can be shot into wooden walls while May gets a hammerhead" that she swings on the nails. "The mechanics and utility of each item are completely different." — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review). (One guide instead put magnets/nails in the Shed. That conflicts with Digital Trends and looks unreliable — [earlyguides](https://earlyguides.pages.dev/it-takes-two/walkthrough).)
- Tree level: Cody shoots sap and May shoots matches that ignite it. One player sets the charge and the other detonates — search excerpts from guides ([earlyguides](https://earlyguides.pages.dev/it-takes-two/walkthrough), [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/ItTakesTwo))
- Clock level: "Cody can temporarily control time, and May" can make copies of herself — [Game Informer cover feature](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- An "attraction" level: "that metaphor for attraction is actually a piece of magnet that we break in two." Each partner holds one magnetic half — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together)
- Pillow Fort: Cody can shrink to fit through small gaps or grow to interact with big objects. Attic: May's voice and Cody's cymbal — guide excerpts ([earlyguides](https://earlyguides.pages.dev/it-takes-two/walkthrough)). These are lower-reliability sources. Some guides also say the two swap abilities mid-chapter.
- Fares on the design goal: find the mechanic that lets both players "feel that they are collaborating." Co-op must be "with you from the beginning in design." — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2021/4/22/22396763/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming)
- Fares gave each Hazelight game one guiding word: "loss" (Brothers), "trust" (A Way Out), "collaboration" (It Takes Two). Communication between players was meant to be essential — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- Split Fiction: Mio and Zoe have "identical jumps and dashes," but the abilities each story gives them "usually differ dramatically." Each level introduces distinct abilities needed to solve puzzles — search excerpts ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/split-fiction-review), [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/6642)). One guide says the sci-fi segments give Mio a grappling hook and Zoe a gravity gun, and the fantasy segments give Mio a blade and Zoe spells. That source is unverified.

**Never Alone (2014). Girl and fox: strength and reach vs agility and the spirit world**
- Nuna climbs ladders and ropes, drags and pushes objects, and throws her bola to break ice and other structures. The fox is fast, climbs walls, slips into small spaces, and draws out spirits that act as platforms for Nuna — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Alone_(video_game)); search excerpts from reviews ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/never-alone-xbox-one-review), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/never-alone-review/))
- The fox also kicks down ropes Nuna can then climb, and distracts enemies. It becomes "a more vital character" as the game goes on — [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/never-alone-xbox-one-review)
- The spirits "only appear when the fox is near them." Nuna's platforms depend on where the fox is — search excerpt ([Never Alone review results](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/never-alone-xbox-one-review))
- Design intent: every element should reflect the values of "interdependence, resiliency, and respect for knowledge passed between generations" (Sean Vesce) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/alaska-natives-share-their-stories-culture-through-commercial-games)

**Little Nightmares III (2025). Bow and wrench**
- Low uses a bow and arrow, and Alone uses a wrench — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nightmares_III). (An Inven interview summary had these the other way round. Wikipedia, the walkthroughs and the reviews all say Low = bow — [Inven](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/19667/little-nightmares-iii-interview-co-op-low-alone-and-the-terrifying-spiral-ahead-of-october-10-release).)
- From reviews: Low's bow hits distant targets, cuts ropes and knocks down flying enemies. Alone's wrench smashes barriers, cranks machinery and crushes stunned enemies — search excerpts from reviews ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/platformer/little-nightmares-3-review), [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/little-nightmares-iii/a-familiar-dream))
- Concrete examples: an arrow carries a rope to make a zipline for Alone. Both grab a plank together to open a door. Low shoots a crow that has the door key in its mouth. The wrench breaks cracked walls, some hiding collectibles. Players hide from a giant baby's spotlight — search excerpts from walkthroughs ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/little-nightmares-3-walkthrough-how-complete-necropolis-chapter-1), [IntoIndieGames](https://intoindiegames.com/walkthroughs/little-nightmares-3-walkthrough-necropolis/))
- Developers: the goal was that "each character always has something meaningful to do," and they "introduced asymmetric gameplay in many puzzles." The pair can call to each other and hold hands — [Inven interview](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/19667/little-nightmares-iii-interview-co-op-low-alone-and-the-terrifying-spiral-ahead-of-october-10-release)
- Critics: a steady rhythm of "you do your thing, I'll do mine." Most puzzles come down to hitting a target with the bow, breaking glass with the wrench, or both pulling a lever or board. Few need both tools at once — search excerpts from several reviews (outlets not individually verified: [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/little-nightmares-3-review-playing-it-safe/), [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/little-nightmares-iii/a-familiar-dream), [Final Boss](https://finalboss.io/little-nightmares-iii-review-eerie-co-op-vibes)). GamesRadar's subtitle: "co-op is a hindrance instead of the evolution it should've been" — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/platformer/little-nightmares-3-review)

**Trine (2009+). Three heroes, physics, many solutions**
- Thief Zoya: a charged bow and a grappling hook that attaches to wooden surfaces. Her flaming arrows light torches and break objects. Wizard Amadeus moves objects remotely and conjures boxes, planks and floating platforms, but cannot attack directly. Knight Pontius: sword and a shield that deflects attacks, falling objects and projectiles, and he can lift and hurl objects — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trine_(video_game))
- "most puzzles and situations can be solved with any character, or any two characters." Switching is never forced. Co-op lets players "have some fun with the physics," for example the Knight throwing a rock another player is standing on — [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-talking-i-trine-i-with-frozenbyte)
- Trine 4: "all puzzles have multiple solutions," but "we've reduced the amount of 'brute-forcing' puzzles overall." Redesigning for multiplayer was hard because there "always needs to be multiple solutions" — search excerpt from [Fandom/Frozenbyte interview](https://www.fandom.com/articles/2017-interview-frozenbyte-on-trine-4-2-5d-is-where)

**Fireboy & Watergirl (2009+). Elemental opposites, one keyboard**
- Only Fireboy can cross lava and only Watergirl can cross water. Touching the other element kills. Green acid kills both. Each collects gems of their own colour. Levers and buttons "must be operated carefully so both characters can finish." Fireboy uses the arrow keys and Watergirl uses WASD — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fireboy_and_Watergirl)
- Albet finalized the mechanics before the characters, and felt two opposing elements were "pretty obvious" for the game to feel natural — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fireboy_and_Watergirl)

**Smaller references**
- Snipperclips (2017): both players are paper shapes that cut each other. "You need sharp corners for some cuts, and a round shape for others." Example: cut a bowl shape to carry balls on your head. The flat tops were kept once the team realized the characters needed to stand on each other — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2017/05/18/afterwords-snipperclips-cut-it-out-together.aspx)
- Rayman Legends (Wii U, 2013): asymmetric roles. One player uses the touch GamePad as Murfy, manipulating the environment for the platforming players — search excerpts ([Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/35322/rayman-legends-wii-u), [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/rayman_legends/b/wii_u/archive/2013/09/03/rayman-hops-from-strength-to-strength))
- Pico Park: tiny characters push boxes, flip switches and carry keys. Puzzles "adjust to suit the number of active players" — search excerpts ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1509960/PICO_PARK), [Shacknews Sonic Pico Park review](https://shacknews.com/article/151275/sonic-pico-park-review-score))
- Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime (2015): players run between ship stations. The developers found that forcing roles (colour-coded power-ups only one player could open) confused players: "We ended up saying 'We'll just let people gravitate to the roles they want to play'" (Adam Winkels) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/co-op-communication-in-i-lovers-in-a-dangerous-spacetime-i-)
- Biped (2020): two robots, each leg on a stick. Reviewers praised the co-op puzzles but split on the controls. "the developers haven't gone quite far enough with pushing the possibilities of their unique control scheme" — search excerpts ([GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/lets-solve-puzzles-together-biped-review/), [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/04/07/biped-review/))

### Inferences
- Three types of "both are needed" recur:
  1. **Body as tool:** Olaf's shield, the partner as anchor in Unravel, stacking in Pico Park and Snipperclips, Brothers' older brother lifting the younger.
  2. **Enabling chain:** A changes the level so B can pass. The zipline arrow, the fox kicking down a rope, Baleog's arrow switch opening Erik's route, Cody's sap waiting for May's match.
  3. **Simultaneous action:** both pull the plank, rope anchor-and-swing, Trine's thrown rock.

  Enabling chains and body-as-tool read as real cooperation. "Parallel gates" (each player clears their own obstacle) is what critics call "you do your thing, I'll do mine."
- For a tall sister and a small brother, the Brothers model maps directly (strong/lift vs squeeze through bars). The Lost Vikings rule ("you can't cross without using each other") is a good acceptance test for every room. If one sibling can finish a room while the other just walks along, it is a parallel gate.
- Ability asymmetry alone does not guarantee interdependence. Little Nightmares III had distinct tools and was still criticized, because the tools rarely interacted. Puzzles need the *outputs* of the two verbs to meet. For example: the sister lifts the brother onto a ledge; he crawls through a vent and unbolts a door for her.
- Unravel Two shows that identical abilities plus a physical link can still make puzzles needing two. But if most rooms can be solved alone, critics notice.

## Key Question 2: Do abilities stay fixed, grow, or change per chapter? What does each choice do to the experience?

### Takeaway
There are three models: **fixed** (Lost Vikings, Brothers, Fireboy & Watergirl, Little Nightmares III), **slow growth/unlocks** (Trine's upgrades, Never Alone's fox gaining spirit-calling), and **replaced every chapter** (It Takes Two, Split Fiction). Fixed abilities make the characters' identities and relationship legible and keep controls simple, but the levels must invent new ways to use the same verbs. Per-chapter replacement gives constant novelty and lets each mechanic be a metaphor. The cost is depth and huge production effort, and some critics call it "kitchen sink" design.

### Cited Findings
- **Fixed (Lost Vikings):** the three Vikings keep their abilities across 37 levels (42 on Genesis), over six worlds in time-travel settings. Variety comes from the setting and the items (keys, bombs, food) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Vikings); [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)
- **Fixed (Brothers):** the brothers' roles never change. Fares relied on situational variety instead: "In Brothers, it was important to change the experience all the time." — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons)
- **Fixed (Little Nightmares III):** one tool each for the whole game, the bow and the wrench — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nightmares_III). Critics said the puzzles felt repetitive (search excerpts in Q1).
- **Fixed but more capable (Unravel Two vs Unravel):** Sahlin said "you're a much more capable character this time around," with faster running, higher jumps and stronger swings. "It's still not a power fantasy." — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=316595)
- **Growth (Trine):** all characters share one experience pool. Every 50 points gives each character an upgrade point, and treasure chests hold charms that grant or improve abilities and can be moved between characters — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trine_(video_game))
- **Growth (Never Alone):** the fox later "gains the ability to call the creatures with a button press," which lets it move spirit "platforms" by hand. The fox becomes "a more vital character" as the game progresses — search excerpt from reviews; [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/never-alone-xbox-one-review)
- **Replaced per level (It Takes Two):** "almost every level in It Takes Two will equip the characters with brand-new mechanics," unlike A Way Out — search excerpt ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/it-takes-two-co-op-gameplay-changes-levels/)). Fares described a mechanic as something "you can only use for that level" — search excerpt (interviews listed at [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/narrative-games-can-become-very-repetitive-josef-fares-promises-max-variety-for-it-takes-two))
- Fares: "I do believe that we need to get away from finding one mechanic and then just repeating it over and over again… every situation the characters are in, you're playing it." Also: "My team is not always happy about this, but they've gotten used to this because I love variation." — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/narrative-games-can-become-very-repetitive-josef-fares-promises-max-variety-for-it-takes-two)
- "repetitiveness is super dangerous" in narrative games. The team aimed for "some kind of world record in the amount of mechanics." The goal was to deliver new ideas quickly rather than have players master mechanics over hours — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together)
- Costs: "Not only do we have to create them, we have to polish them" (Fares) — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2021/4/22/22396763/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming). In Split Fiction, "just one of those dragons took, I think, eight months to create" — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/03/05/split-fiction-josef-fares-interview/)
- A transcript of a Hazelight developer discussion says each level got its own rule set and internal logic, compared to teaching a player a new game in an hour. Each mechanic was paired with a setting the art team had prototyped — search excerpts from an Amara-hosted transcript ([amara.org](https://amara.org/videos/tUIr67mipkE6/en/3705993)); the full transcript could not be fetched
- Downside per Digital Trends: the puzzles are "rarely challenging." Its only listed con is "Kitchen sink design." "There are a lot of rapid-fire gameplay ideas happening at every level that don't always sync up to a relationship parallel." — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review)
- Split Fiction hybrid: shared base movement (jump, dash) plus per-level dramatically different abilities — search excerpt ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/split-fiction-review))

### Inferences
- The project's pillars (a tall sister reaches high; a small brother fits through gaps) fit the **fixed identity + per-chapter "gadget" layer** hybrid that Split Fiction uses. Keep the core sibling verbs permanent, because they are who the characters are. Add one temporary chapter tool or vehicle per set piece: the ferry, the metro, the lift bridge, the waterfall. That gets novelty without blurring the characters.
- Fixed abilities with no added layer (Little Nightmares III) need much more inventive level design to avoid repetition. Brothers got away with it because its game is short (about 3 hours, not sourced here) and changes situation constantly.
- If jumping is added, The Lost Vikings is a cautionary precedent: making jump exclusive to one character (Erik) creates strong gates. Giving both characters jump erases a whole class of "lift me up" puzzles. The Brothers model (no free jump, context climbing and lifting) keeps the sister's reach meaningful.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how long players spend per mechanic in It Takes Two.
- No reliable data on whether players preferred fixed or changing abilities (no surveys found).

## Key Question 3: How is each new mechanic introduced and taught without text, and how does complexity escalate across chapters?

### Takeaway
Sources mostly describe teaching implicitly: a mechanic first appears in a safe, isolated space, then combines with a hazard or the partner's verb, and the camera and level shape point at the solution. The best-documented lessons are negative. Blizzard saw a child quit after a first-level electric pit and resolved to make onboarding "as safe and easy as possible." Never Alone's difficulty "spikes more significantly than we intended" at the end. Brothers deliberately kept every puzzle "soft." Explicit hint systems appear in Unravel Two (three tiers). Hazelight compresses learning so a new rule set is learned and exhausted within one level.

### Cited Findings
- **Safe onboarding (Blizzard):** Allen Adham watched a young player quit on the first level after falling into an electricity pit. This shaped Blizzard's goal of making onboarding "as safe and easy as possible" so players feel powerful from the start — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)
- **Brothers keeps puzzles gentle:** early puzzles were too hard, so "Every puzzle and challenge needed to be quite soft and not too hard." "It was very important to me to keep the pace going." — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons). Reviewers called the puzzles "easy, but incredibly intuitive" and "none of them are particularly obscure" — search excerpts from Brothers reviews (e.g. [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/brothers-tale-two-sons-review/))
- **No readable language in Brothers:** all dialogue is in an invented language based on Lebanese Arabic, so story and instruction come through action, gesture and expression — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers:_A_Tale_of_Two_Sons). Early tests with inexperienced players found the two-stick scheme easier to learn than an FPS layout. A camera keeping both brothers on screen helped — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons); search excerpt ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-03-13-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-director-on-next-project-those-con.html))
- **Hazelight compression:** each level is its own rule set, "like teaching a player a new game in an hour" — search excerpt ([amara.org transcript](https://amara.org/videos/tUIr67mipkE6/en/3705993)). Fares wants new ideas delivered quickly, not mastered over hours — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together)
- **Within-level escalation (It Takes Two):** in the opening level the nail/hammer pair escalates into a boss: a broken vacuum becomes "a Bowser-sized boss." A wasp nest turns into "a mini third-person shooter." When a platforming idea starts to feel stale the game "swaps over to a mini-dungeon crawler" — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review)
- **Escalation by numbers (Fireboy & Watergirl):** Forest Temple has 32 levels "of increasing difficulty" (search excerpt). GamerBolt called the introductory levels "too-easy" before the challenge rises. Later entries move to new settings (forest, light, ice, crystal) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fireboy_and_Watergirl)
- **Escalation and AI scaffolding (Never Alone):** the companion AI "always tries to get to the player-controlled character." Designers added jump and ledge triggers, then mid-project "beacon triggers" that make the AI wait at a spot until conditions are met. Partner positioning was hand-authored per puzzle — [Game Developer postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-e-line-media-and-upper-one-games-i-never-alone-i-)
- **Never Alone difficulty curve went wrong:** "The difficulty level in the game spikes more significantly than we intended towards the end." — [Game Developer postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-e-line-media-and-upper-one-games-i-never-alone-i-). A review: "a reliance on trial and error and expert timing make the solo experience more fraught with frustration" — [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/never-alone-xbox-one-review)
- **Unravel Two:** main levels are easier, partly because of co-op and a three-stage hint system. Separate challenge levels test reflexes and puzzle-solving — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=316595). Challenge levels unlock after the regular levels and "offer a deeper dive into the game mechanics" — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/you-and-me-till-the-end-unravel-two-review/)
- **Trine:** the aim is to "challenge the player but not to frustrate." The level designers "have a lot of freedom to create what they want and then we just play the levels a lot." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-talking-i-trine-i-with-frozenbyte). One critic thought Trine 2's puzzles became "more nefarious," with specific solutions players are "not invited to deviate from" — search excerpt (Trine 2 reviews, e.g. [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/trine-2-review-pc))
- **Snipperclips iteration:** players kept reusing one shape (a scoop), so the designers focused new puzzles on forcing different shapes — search excerpt ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/11/feature_sfb_games_on_going_back_for_more_in_snipperclips_plus_cut_it_out_together))
- **Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime:** single-player with an AI pet was used to tune co-op difficulty. If experts could direct the pet through a scenario, newer human players could usually survive it too. "Building the single player mode informed our ability to do co-op design." — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/co-op-communication-in-i-lovers-in-a-dangerous-spacetime-i-)

### Inferences
- A workable recipe from these sources:
  1. Introduce each sibling verb where failure costs nothing (Blizzard's lesson).
  2. Next, put the verb where the *other* sibling's verb is needed to use it (Unravel's anchor-and-swing, Never Alone's fox-near-spirit).
  3. Then combine it with a moving element or a timer in a set piece (It Takes Two's level-ending boss).
  4. End chapters on a "remix" that uses all earlier verbs.
- Never Alone's end-of-game spike is a warning. For a family audience, Chapter 3 (forest) should escalate by *combining* known verbs, not by demanding precision or timing. Brothers' "soft" puzzles were praised for pacing and criticized only as "overly simple."
- Snipperclips' lesson applies to split abilities: watch for one dominant solution (e.g. "sister lifts brother" solving everything). Design rooms where that default fails.
- Never Alone's "beacon triggers" show that a partner's waiting position is part of puzzle design. For online co-op the matching tool is hand-placed "wait here" spots that frame where the second player should stand.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that describes a specific tutorial room step by step (e.g. Unravel Two's first tether lesson or It Takes Two's first nail/hammer room) from the developers. Game Maker's Toolkit's "What Makes a Good Puzzle?" exists ([amara.org](https://amara.org/v/C3BEg/)), but no GMTK video specifically on Brothers, It Takes Two or co-op puzzle teaching was found.
- No source on how Pico Park or Fireboy & Watergirl introduce new elements (e.g. mirrors, portals) level by level.

## Key Question 4: How do puzzles connect to story events (puzzle as story beat, set pieces, relationship changes), and how do the games vary pace (rest, spectacle, mini-games)?

### Takeaway
The strongest examples make the *control scheme or ability itself* carry the story. Brothers is the canonical case: the younger brother's fear of water is overcome near the end by using the dead older brother's input. Hazelight maps mechanics to emotional states (a magnet broken in two for lost attraction, time control and self-cloning for an over-busy marriage). Never Alone rewards play with documentary videos that explain the myth being played. Pace is varied by short-lived genre shifts, optional competitive mini-games, quiet optional interactions (Brothers' benches and flowers), and expensive one-off set pieces deliberately never reused.

### Cited Findings

**Brothers: the control scheme as story**
- Fares: losing a brother also takes away a control hand, which makes it "even more odd that that hand is gone." The aim was "that feeling that you're missing something." — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons)
- The swim alone was an early idea. The "essence of the whole game" is pushing the button to take strength from the older brother: "by pushing the button and getting the strength from him, that was such a hard moment, and it felt so good." The burial is interactive: "You actually carry your brother into the hole." — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons)
- The story ending: Naia dies from a wound from a spider creature near the Tree of Life. Naiee collects the Water of Life, buries his brother, and swims to the village to save their father — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers:_A_Tale_of_Two_Sons)
- An archive page says Fares called "controlling with two sticks" and the ending the foundation of the game from the start. That is a secondary source — search excerpt ([archania.org](https://archania.org/p/the-symbolic-world/symbolic-works/video-games/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons))
- Because the ending depends on one player holding both sticks, Fares refused co-op ("I would rather cut my hands than do that") and called it "a single-player co-op game." — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons); [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/81361/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-not-meant-for-co)
- 2024 remake: it adds couch co-op with two controllers. Reviews were split: Press Start called local co-op "a rather special addition," while Game8 listed "co-op is not the intended way to play" as a con. Several still recommend solo. The story is reported unchanged from 2013. WellPlayed's headline: "Ending still flounders" — search excerpts ([Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/xbox-series-x-reviews/2024/02/28/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake-review/), [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake-review), [WellPlayed](https://www.well-played.com.au/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake-review/)). I could not verify how the remake's co-op handles the ending's control twist.
- Optional "quiet" interactions express character: the flower, and the hint that "a lot of people have missed a lot." — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons)

**It Takes Two / Split Fiction: mechanics as metaphor, set pieces never reused**
- "We tried to marry those two, so they're connected to their emotional states as well." "Because we're doing a narrative game, every situation has to reflect what goes on in the game." — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together)
- Examples: a magnet broken in two (attraction); Cody's time control and May's clones (being stretched too thin) — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together). Gardening and singing levels reflect Cody's and May's neglected passions — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- "If you meet a couple of squirrels with some weird tic, you play that." On a plane fight: "there's a huge amount of time put into that, and you only play it for one minute." Replaying it "takes away the specialness of it." — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2021/4/22/22396763/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming)
- Split Fiction: "cool moments like that wouldn't have been as cool if we just reused them all the time." Side stories are "full-blown worlds with new mechanics, sometimes bosses… almost like a new game inside the game." — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/03/05/split-fiction-josef-fares-interview/)
- Side Stories (about 12, 5–10 minutes each) are optional portals based on Mio's and Zoe's past writing. Some segments switch to a 2D side-scroller — search excerpts ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/action/split-fictions-latest-trailer-shows-off-its-side-stories-which-let-you-fly-through-space-and-ride-giant-desert-fish-in-worlds-completely-different-from-the-main-adventure), [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/6642), [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/split-fiction-review))
- Process: the team storyboarded scenes before building much gameplay, then adjusted story and gameplay together. The tone was a romantic comedy inspired by Pixar — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- Pacing tools: optional competitive mini-games on short side paths that track wins between the players (tug of war, snail racing) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game)); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review). Toys with no reward, like a paper airplane — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review). Genre shifts (shooter, dungeon crawler, musical set piece). Nine levels over 10–12 hours — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review)
- Criticism: plot threads sometimes "come out of left field to justify moving the game to a snow-themed level." The story "can get narratively scattershot" — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review)
- A GDC session, "Designing the Final Level of Split Fiction," covers how that level's gimmick "turned out to be the largest co-op design challenge so far" — search excerpt ([GDC schedule](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/designing-the-final-level-of-split-fiction/917468)); page not fetchable

**Never Alone: gameplay unlocks the culture**
- Cultural Insights are short video interviews with Iñupiaq elders, storytellers and community members, collected while playing (over 40 hours of footage shot). - Local co-op was chosen partly because talking through puzzles over a headset would break immersion, and because the game "requires such specific timing." — [Game Developer postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-e-line-media-and-upper-one-games-i-never-alone-i-)

**Unravel Two: a background story the players pass through**
- Ghostly children appear as "wispy figures acting out small vignettes in their lives together," for example fleeing someone who means to reprimand them — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/you-and-me-till-the-end-unravel-two-review/). Sahlin: "There is a little bit of a parallel story in this game as well," and "the little spark that you're chasing splits into two parts" when people are overwhelmed — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=316595)
- Plot: the two Yarnys "connect their dead ends together, forming a bond." They chase a spark through scenes of children escaping abusive adults, a factory and a forest fire, and finally restore a lighthouse (the hub) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unravel_Two)

**Little Nightmares III: holding hands as the relationship verb**
- The pair can call to each other and hold hands. Developers wanted a deeper emotional bond, "two friends, helping each other" — [Inven](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/19667/little-nightmares-iii-interview-co-op-low-alone-and-the-terrifying-spiral-ahead-of-october-10-release); search excerpt ([OneMoreGame](https://onemoregame.ph/2024/10/little-nightmares-iii-interview-producer/))
- Co-op is online only, so neither player "feel[s] too safe" and wide panoramic shots (e.g. the Candy Factory) are kept. Director Wayne Garland said local co-op would have taken too much from "atmosphere and immersion" — search excerpts ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/little-nightmares-3-developer-explains-online-only-co-op-3492181), [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/little-nightmares-3-devs-decided-to-not-include-couch-co-op-to-maintain-atmosphere-and-immersion))

**The Lost Vikings and Snipperclips: humour as glue**
- Critics praised "the funny (and sometimes helpful) dialogue" between levels — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Vikings). The setting changes across time periods — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)
- Snipperclips centres the players, not the characters: the story focus is "on the two people who are actually playing the game together." — [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2017/05/18/afterwords-snipperclips-cut-it-out-together.aspx)
- Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime: "It's absolutely a microcosm for the relationship of the 2 people playing our game." — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/co-op-communication-in-i-lovers-in-a-dangerous-spacetime-i-)

### Inferences
- The Brothers ending works because the game spent hours teaching that each hand *is* a sibling. A single late twist on an established verb carries more emotion than any cutscene. For the siblings, a payoff could invert a role. For example, the small brother finally reaches something high with a trick the sister taught him, or the sister must trust the brother to go somewhere she cannot follow. This is a pattern, not Brothers' specific content.
- In online co-op, two players each hold one sibling, so the Brothers "one player, two hands" trick cannot be copied directly. The equivalents are role inversion and having one player wait on the other's input. Brothers' own remake adds co-op, and reviewers felt that weakened it.
- Hazelight's "mechanic = metaphor" fits the treasure-map premise. Each chapter's set piece (ferry, metro, lift bridge, waterfall) can embody the siblings' relationship at that point, e.g. learning to trust in the city, then working in sync in the forest.
- Pacing tools that fit a family-friendly browser game: optional sibling-vs-sibling mini-games (It Takes Two), reward-free toys, optional character-revealing interactions (Brothers), and spectacle set pieces used once and never repeated.
- Never Alone shows that reward content can be *meaning* (cultural insight) rather than points. For a storybook game, map pages or illustrated snippets unlocked at story beats could do the same job.

## Key Question 5: What do these games do with failure, checkpoints, and hints?

### Takeaway
Most use frequent checkpoints with quick, cheap respawns. It Takes Two lets a downed player mash a button to return faster. Trine's checkpoints revive all heroes. Brothers restarts at a nearby checkpoint. Games that make failure costly (The Lost Vikings, where one dead Viking makes the level unwinnable; Pico Park, where one slip restarts everyone) turn failure into shared, often comedic tension. Explicit hints are rare. Unravel Two's tiered three-step hint system is the clearest model. Snipperclips designs failure to be funny.

### Cited Findings
- **It Takes Two:** characters respawn quickly. In harder sections (bosses) the defeated player can speed up their return by repeatedly pressing a button — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- **Brothers:** a fall from a great height or an injury restarts at a recent checkpoint — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers:_A_Tale_of_Two_Sons)
- **Trine:** silver orbs are checkpoints that revive dead characters and restore health and energy. If all three die, the player returns to the last checkpoint. The goal is "to challenge the player but not to frustrate" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trine_(video_game)); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-talking-i-trine-i-with-frozenbyte)
- **The Lost Vikings:** each Viking has three health points. Running out makes the level unwinnable, with unlimited continues — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Vikings). Blizzard cut an auto-follow feature because a character dying "due to being auto-following someone else" would be frustrating — [Blizzard News](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles)
- **Unravel Two:** a three-stage hint system — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=316595). It runs "from a slight thought-provoking prod to a detailed explanation," described as "a very generous hint system that will slowly spell out the solution" — search excerpts from Unravel Two reviews ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/unravel-two-review), [Vooks](https://vooks.net/unravel-two-switch-review); outlet not verified). A struggling player can also merge into their partner so the other "carry[s] the duo through a tricky situation" — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/you-and-me-till-the-end-unravel-two-review/)
- **Little Nightmares (series):** in Little Nightmares II, small mistakes were often fatal (Inven's framing). III's difficulty is "similar to past entries" — [Inven](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/19667/little-nightmares-iii-interview-co-op-low-alone-and-the-terrifying-spiral-ahead-of-october-10-release)
- **Pico Park:** "wait until one player trips, and everyone restarts from the beginning!" Failing "spectacularly is just as fun as solving the puzzles" — search excerpts ([Wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/game/pico-park-1509960), [Typelish](https://typelish.com/b/teamwork-chaos-and-cats-pico-park-113338))
- **Snipperclips:** SFB's goal was that messing up should often be funnier than the right solution (Tom Vian) — search excerpt ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/03/feature_snipperclips_-_working_with_nintendo_to_create_a_switch_co-op_classic))
- **Fireboy & Watergirl:** after each level a chart shows gems collected and a rank — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fireboy_and_Watergirl). That gives a mastery target beyond simply finishing.
- **Never Alone:** "trial and error and expert timing" made solo play frustrating — [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/never-alone-xbox-one-review)

### Inferences
- For a family audience playing online: use frequent checkpoints, respawn one sibling without resetting the other's progress where possible (It Takes Two style), and keep "both restart" resets (Pico Park) for short, comic rooms.
- A tiered hint (nudge → stronger cue → show) fits the "no text" pillar if expressed visually: a glint on the high shelf, the brother pointing at the vent, then a ghost-silhouette demonstration. Unravel Two's approach is the model.
- Snipperclips' "failure is funny" suits family play. Sibling-specific fail animations (the brother bonking his head, the sister fumbling) can turn retries into story moments.

### Gaps
- No source found on how Little Nightmares III's online co-op handles one player dying (whether both reset).
- No source on whether Brothers or Never Alone offer any hint system.

## Key Question 6: What do reviewers, developers and players say worked or did not work?

### Takeaway
What worked: interdependence written into the rules (Lost Vikings), a control scheme that carries emotion (Brothers), a constant flow of new mechanics tied to theme (It Takes Two: Game of the Year at TGA 2021 and DICE, over 30 million copies by April 2026), and meaningful non-gameplay rewards (Never Alone's videos). What failed: AI companions (Never Alone, Rayman Murfy solo), parallel instead of interdependent puzzles (Little Nightmares III, Unravel Two), difficulty spikes (Never Alone), weak solo modes, and short length (Biped, Unravel Two at seven story levels).

### Cited Findings
- **It Takes Two:** reviewers praised the co-op design, mechanical variety and link between gameplay and story. GameSpot said frequent new ideas kept it from stagnating. Game of the Year at The Game Awards 2021 and the 25th DICE Awards. Over 1 million units in the first month, 7 million by mid-2022, over 20 million by late 2024, over 30 million by April 2026. Criticism targeted the script and Dr. Hakim (PC Gamer) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- Fares does not design to playtester requests: "We do the game we want to do," and he wants players "to like the game we're doing, not adjust the game for what they want." The 51% completion rate for A Way Out "saddens me." — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together). He avoids collectibles and replay hooks: "you don't have any collectibles in It Takes Two" — search excerpt ([Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/features/2021/03/03/we-spoke-to-josef-fares-about-it-takes-two-and-the-industrys-obsession-with-replayability/))
- **Brothers:** DICE Downloadable Game of the Year, VGX Best Xbox Game, BAFTA Best Game Innovation 2014. Over 800,000 sold by Jan 2015. IGN: "might be the best downloadable title since Journey" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers:_A_Tale_of_Two_Sons). Criticism: "overly simple," and much of the game is "travelling across the various landscapes" — search excerpts from Brothers reviews
- **Never Alone:** "Nearly every review mentioned 'the AI' as a negative." The team was "completely blindsided." Their recommendation: a closed beta for "fresh eyes on the game and fresh hands on the controller." A cut milestone (a nonlinear village, object lifting, a fishing mini-game) is called "The Dark Ages" — [Game Developer postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-e-line-media-and-upper-one-games-i-never-alone-i-). Metacritic "mixed or average." Critics praised the art and videos and criticized imprecise controls. It won the BAFTA for Best Debut and Games for Change Game of the Year, and entered MoMA's collection in 2022 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Alone_(video_game)). Game Informer: "touchy controls, questionable A.I., and dull puzzle design" — search excerpt ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/never_alone/b/pc/archive/2014/12/16/never-alone-review-game-informer.aspx))
- **Unravel Two:** DICE Family Game of the Year. Local co-op only — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unravel_Two). Criticisms: "a much less enjoyable single-player mode" and a "fairly short game at only seven story levels"; verdict 85 — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/you-and-me-till-the-end-unravel-two-review/). One critic said the tether makes agency "more of a frustration than a freedom" — search excerpt (Unravel Two reviews)
- **Little Nightmares III:** Metacritic 69–72, OpenCritic 61% recommend. Critics "praised the game's atmosphere and visuals but criticized the gameplay and technical issues." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nightmares_III). Slow movement made climbing, jumping and box-pushing "feel unnecessarily sluggish," and there is no local co-op — search excerpts from LN3 reviews. Puzzles were "accessible… impossible to solve solo" — search excerpt
- **Trine:** IGN 8.2 but criticized the multiplayer. Metacritic around 80 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trine_(video_game)). Physics made it hard to test: "Trine isn't the easiest game to test due to the physics and unlimited gameplay possibilities." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-talking-i-trine-i-with-frozenbyte)
- **Rayman Legends:** Murfy is called "the most well-implemented use of the GamePad to date," but alone "the A.I. simply fails" to adjust to your mistakes — search excerpts ([Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/35322/rayman-legends-wii-u), [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/rayman_legends/b/wii_u/archive/2013/09/03/rayman-hops-from-strength-to-strength))
- **Biped:** praised for co-op, criticized for controls and length ("as you really begin to get a feel for one puzzle, it ends"). ### Inferences
- Never Alone's postmortem and the Rayman and Biped reviews agree: when a partner (AI or human) cannot cover for your mistakes, frustration rises. Online co-op with two humans avoids AI problems. It adds a need for in-game communication (Little Nightmares III's call and hold-hand, Pico Park's shouting). Since the user's game has *split information*, voiceless signals (point, call, ping) will carry a lot of weight.
- Little Nightmares III's reception suggests that tools which only ever affect "their own" obstacles feel thin. Interlocking verbs and the occasional simultaneous-action moment do better with critics.
- Hazelight's "never reuse" approach succeeded commercially, but it is the most expensive pattern documented here (eight months for one dragon). A small team gets more out of a few deep sibling verbs plus a handful of one-off set pieces.
