# Puzzle Mechanics Catalogue for a Two-Character Forest / Waterfall Co-op Puzzle Game

Scope note: these notes collect puzzle-mechanic patterns from side-scrolling and 2D (plus a few 3D reference) puzzle games. They are meant as inspiration for an online, browser-based, 2-player, family-friendly side-view game. The players are an older sister (tall, reaches high) and a younger brother (small, fits gaps, crawls). Current verbs are walk, stairs, crawl and press/hold. Jumping is undecided. The research was done in November 2026. Cited Findings give what sources say. Inferences give my design suggestions for this game, which are not facts from sources. Several citations come from review excerpts surfaced by search rather than full-page reads (see Gaps in section 1).

## 1. What puzzle mechanic families exist, how does each work, and which games use them well?

### Takeaway
The strongest reference for this exact game is Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons. Its older brother pulls levers and boosts, and its younger brother slips through bars and is carried through water. Never Alone (girl and fox), Thomas Was Alone (tall, small and floating blocks) and Machinarium (stretch and squash to reach high or low) confirm that a tall/small split can carry a whole game. For nature settings, Seasons After Fall (season-driven water and plants), Max: The Curse of Brotherhood (grow pillars, branches and vines, steer water), Toki Tori 2 (whistle or stomp to steer animals) and Planet of Lana I and II (control creatures, melodies, diving with a companion) offer the richest mechanic sets. The Witness shows how to hide patterns in trees, shadows and birdsong.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Split-ability pairs (the backbone of a sister/brother design)
- Brothers: one controller, with each stick driving one brother: "one stick for Naia, the big brother, and one for Naiee, the younger." — [Medium analysis](https://medium.com/rough-draft-media-story-and-society/how-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-ses-the-controller-to-tell-a-story-75ec04841591)
- Brothers: the older brother is stronger. He pulls levers and boosts his younger brother to higher spaces, and the younger one passes between narrow bars. — [WalkthroughKing](https://www.walkthroughking.com/text/brothers.aspx)
- Brothers, concrete examples: (a) the little brother goes through gate bars to turn a crank so the big brother can come through. (b) The big brother must "pull and hold the lever" to open a cage while the little one slips inside. (c) In the remake, the little brother turns a lever to open a gate and must keep holding "until Big Brother is through, as the gate will fall if you stop turning." — [WalkthroughKing](https://www.walkthroughking.com/text/brothers.aspx); [Ludo.guide part 8](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-part-8); [Ludo.guide remake part 6](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake-part-6)
- Brothers: in water, the little brother latches onto the big brother and is carried while the big brother swims. — [WalkthroughKing](https://www.walkthroughking.com/text/brothers.aspx)
- Brothers: puzzles were "kept fairly gentle, and designed to feel more comfortable when controlling two rather than one." — [Epic Games Store on the remake](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-the-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake-reimagines-an-indie-classic)
- Never Alone: Nuna climbs ladders and ropes, shifts heavy objects and throws a bola at targets. Fox fits through small gaps Nuna can't, climbs walls and jumps high. — [Nintendo listing](https://nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-download-software/Never-Alone-Arctic-Collection-2172916.html)
- Never Alone: Fox draws out helping spirits, which then serve as platforms and ladders for Nuna. — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/never_alone)
- Thomas Was Alone: Chris is small with a weak jump but fits through gaps. John is tall and jumps much higher. Claire is large with a low jump but floats on water. Laura is a block other characters bounce off. — [TrueAchievements guide](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Thomas-Was-Alone/walkthrough/2); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Was_Alone)
- Thomas Was Alone: the designer kept characters rectangular partly to keep the stacking mechanic simple. Blocks with different abilities that must cooperate were meant to represent friendship. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Was_Alone)
- Machinarium: Josef can squash or stretch his body "enabling him to reach higher or lower points of interest." He can only interact with objects within his physical reach. Walkthrough examples: stretch to reach a lever in the Furnace Room, or crouch through crawlspaces beneath the Bar. — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/machinarium); [Hardcore Gaming 101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/machinarium/); [Cyberly walkthrough](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/machinarium-walkthrough.html)
- Trine 4: the Wizard conjures boxes and levitates props. The Thief has a grappling rope, ice arrows and bridges. The Knight reflects things off his shield and charges walls. In co-op, "the Wizard can levitate a box with the Knight standing on it, while the Thief dangles beneath." — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/trine); [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/trine-4-review)
- It Takes Two, "The Tree" chapter: Cody's sap gun weighs platforms and objects down to activate platforms or doors. May's match weapon detonates the sap. Sources disagree on May's weapon name ("Drill Buzzer" match rifle versus "rocket launcher"). — [Gamepur](https://www.gamepur.com/?p=152294); [SegmentNext](https://segmentnext.com/it-takes-two-the-tree)
- It Takes Two: an MDA analysis says each player gets different tools that must be combined, so neither player can advance alone. — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2025/04/08/spencer-mda-8-kinds-of-fun/)
- Fireboy and Watergirl (Forest Temple): levers stay in place once pulled, while buttons only work while something presses them. Boxes are pushed. Colored gems can be collected only by the matching character. Box placement near water or fire hints at which character should use it. Sources are unofficial browser-game portals. — [Agame](https://www.kids.agame.com/game/fireboy-and-watergirl-the-forest-temple); [Numuki](https://www.numuki.com/game/the-forest-temple)
- Little Nightmares III (2025): reviewers say the game has many puzzles built for two, with "one can't progress without the other." One reviewer felt that making co-op optional limited the puzzle design. Co-op is online-only, with no local co-op. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/platformer/little-nightmares-3-review); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/little-nightmares-iii); [FingerGuns](https://fingerguns.net/reviews/2025/10/14/little-nightmares-iii-review-ps5-wake-me-up-when-its-all-over/)

#### B. Weight, balance, carrying and stacking
- It Takes Two: one sibling shoots sap to weigh down one side of a wheel so it rotates from a "plus sign to an X," and the other then crosses. In a second puzzle, one player rotates yellow X-shaped platforms by shooting fragments while the other hops between them. — [Gamepur](https://www.gamepur.com/?p=152294)
- Pico Park: standing on another player's head is the simplest help. The bottom player holds still while those above move to reach high platforms. In key-and-door levels, players push platforms, then one jumps onto the other's head to grab the key. — [Typelish review](https://typelish.com/b/teamwork-chaos-and-cats-pico-park-113338); [Daily Campus](https://dailycampus.com/2023/11/14/the-backlog-destroy-your-friendships-with-pico-park/)
- Thomas Was Alone: stacking blocks with different abilities is the core mechanic. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Was_Alone)
- Trine: levitating a box while another character rides it (see A). — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/trine)
- Fireboy and Watergirl: held buttons versus latching levers, with elevators and platforms driven by buttons or levers. — [Agame](https://www.kids.agame.com/game/fireboy-and-watergirl-the-forest-temple)

#### C. Water
- Seasons After Fall: spring rain fills lakes and raises them, and summer heat dries and lowers them. Winter freezes water, including small geysers, into stepping stones. A common combined puzzle: release water from giant plants to raise a pond, then freeze it to reach a high place. Many puzzles require interacting in one season and then doubling back. — [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/seasons-after-fall-review/); [Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/xbox-one/seasons-after-fall-2); [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=276105)
- Zelda: Ocarina of Time Water Temple, the classic water-level dungeon. Water can be set to three heights from a limited number of spots. Critics call the resulting backtracking "exhausting repetition." Defenders call it a masterclass that is less confusing than it first seems. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Temple_(Ocarina_of_Time)); [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=11084); [Haptic Feedback analysis](https://hapticfeedbackgames.blogspot.com/2014/10/water-temple-analysis-part-1.html)
- Water Temple critique: one analysis argues that letting players change the water in more places, or choose a height directly, would have cut cycling through the same rooms. — [Goomba Stomp](https://goombastomp.com/?p=114502)
- A GamingBolt article dated November 2026 reports Zelda producer Eiji Aonuma calling improvement of the Water Temple "overdue homework." Only the opening was seen. — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=656348)
- Ori and the Blind Forest, Ginso Tree: after the tree is restored, Ori escapes upward as water rises from below. It is a long multi-room run with no save point, and Easy Mode adds checkpoints. — [Xbox Wire 2014](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2014/08/14/gamescom-ori-preview/); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/OriAndTheBlindForest)
- Planet of Lana II (2026): Lana can dive, and breath creates time pressure underwater. Mui fears water, so Lana fetches a bubble plant and ties it to herself, and Mui rides inside the bubble so the pair can solve underwater puzzles. A controllable ink fish fits narrow tunnels, leaps out of water to reach new areas, and makes an ink cloud that hides it and Lana from sharks. — [Xbox Wire, Mar 2026](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- Planet of Lana (first game): hypnotized swamp larvae "suck the water out of the pond." — [A Sound Effect interview](https://www.asoundeffect.com/planet-of-lana-game-audio/)
- Max: The Curse of Brotherhood: the marker draws water streams that move objects and Max along their path. Grown branches can serve as rafts. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/max-the-curse-of-brotherhood-review/); [Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/max_the_curse_of_brotherhood)
- Unravel (first game): water is the main hazard, and Yarny drowns quickly. Fish can pull Yarny across water. These are review excerpts surfaced by search, and the exact outlet among those listed was not confirmed. — [Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/unravel-review/); [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/games/ps4-games/1404485/unravel-review-beautiful-but-straightforward)
- Thomas Was Alone: only Claire floats, so she ferries others across water. — [TrueAchievements guide](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Thomas-Was-Alone/walkthrough/2)

#### D. Light and shadow
- Fireboy and Watergirl 2: Light Temple: mirrors are rotated to redirect beams into receivers that open gates or raise platforms. Movable blocks carry mirrors that reflect light 90 degrees. An unofficial tips page says prism levels need both beams on target at once. — [Jayisgames review](https://Jayisgames.com/review/fireboy-and-watergirl-2-the-light-temple.php); [unofficial tips page](https://fireboyandwatergirl-eip.pages.dev/blog/fireboy-watergirl-2-light-temple-tips-mastering-mirror-puzzles)
- Rayman Legends: a second player controls Murfy by touchscreen to manipulate the stage, for example holding an object in front of a rotating floodlight to cast shadows. — [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayman_Legends)
- Projection: First Light: one stick moves the girl Greta and the other moves a light. The light casts shadow platforms Greta crosses. Reviewers found shadows "finicky" in timed puzzles. — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/reviews/projection-first-light-review); [Hooked Gamers](https://hookedgamers.com/pc/projection_first_light/review/article-2213.html)
- The Witness: the "Shady Trees" area makes players read tree shadows and light through the canopy to solve grid puzzles. Some panels need water reflections. Environmental puzzles are hidden in clouds, cast shadows and tree-canopy outlines. — [GameRevolution guide](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=12219); [Straight Dope forum thread](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/the-witness-puzzle-game/1005336?page=11); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/great-moments-in-pc-gaming-spotting-your-first-environmental-puzzle-in-the-witness)
- Ori, Misty Woods: a dark, maze-like area shrouded in mist. Its "Shrouded Lantern" is found without light, and Ori must bring a torch back to light it, which straightens the maze. In the Forlorn Ruins, a "Light Shield" orb can be carried and dropped, and it follows Ori when not held. — [Gamer Walkthroughs](https://gamerwalkthroughs.com/ori-and-the-blind-forest/); [Gamer Walkthroughs, Forlorn Ruins](https://gamerwalkthroughs.com/ori-and-the-blind-forest/forlorn-ruins/)
- Child of Light: an optional second player controls Igniculus, a firefly who reveals hidden passages, disables traps and heals. A co-op review says the puzzle role "tapers off severely." — [Co-Optimus game page](https://www.co-optimus.com/game/3132/platform/game.html); [Co-Optimus review](https://co-optimus.com/review/1365/page/2/child-of-light-co-op-review.html)
- Botanicula: one of the five heroes is a little lantern seed. — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/botanicula/b/pc/archive/2012/04/30/a-world-of-wonder-and-weirdness)

#### E. Plants and growth
- Max: The Curse of Brotherhood: the marker raises earth pillars from marked spots that glow orange. It grows branches that serve as platforms, springboards, rafts and movable objects, and branches burn. It grows vines that work like ropes to climb, swing and tie objects together. Powers combine, for example a vine attached to an earth pillar or branch to make a bridge. Reviewers found the drawing controls imprecise. — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/max_the_curse_of_brotherhood/b/xboxone/archive/2013/12/20/the-novelty-is-drying-out); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/max-the-curse-of-brotherhood-review/); [Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/max_the_curse_of_brotherhood)
- Seasons After Fall: plants behave by season. Bulb plants spit snowballs in winter and water in spring, which lets trees grow. Trees grow tall in spring, and some plants extend in summer. — [Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/xbox-one/seasons-after-fall-2); [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/seasons-after-fall-review/)
- Unravel: a knot tied between nearby anchor points becomes a bridge that holds movable objects, or a trampoline that launches Yarny. This is a review excerpt, and the outlet was not confirmed. — [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/games/ps4-games/1404485/unravel-review-beautiful-but-straightforward); [Wikipedia](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Unravel_(video_game))
- Kena: Bridge of Spirits: small "Rot" spirits are ordered to move objects in a Pikmin-like way. GameSpot says the puzzles mostly reward observation. — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/reviews/kena-bridge-of-spirits-review-dont-fear-the-reaper/1900-6417744/); [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=693266)
- Botanicula: the heroes are a seed, a stick, a feather, a mushroom and a fungus. One can fly and the stick insect stretches to reach objects. Reviewers say these abilities are used "only sporadically." — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/botanicula/b/pc/archive/2012/04/30/a-world-of-wonder-and-weirdness); [OutNow](https://outnow.ch/en/Games/2012/Botanicula/Review/)

#### F. Animals (lure, scare, ride, control, avoid)
- Toki Tori 2: there are only two actions. Whistle draws creatures closer, and stomp pushes them away and breaks certain terrain. Block-carrying crabs can be called or sent away. In one example, a stomp drops a berry to a lower area and a whistle attracts a frog to eat it. There are "no words, no tutorial and no UI overlay," and later whistle "songs" add specialist effects. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Tori_2); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/toki_tori_2); [Snackbar Games](https://www.snackbar-games.com/reviews/toki-tori-2-a-minimalist-puzzling-masterpiece)
- Planet of Lana II: the companion Mui hypnotizes creatures, and the player then directly controls them and uses their abilities. The designers wanted "the player to instead become the animal." — [GamingBolt interview](https://gamingbolt.com/planet-of-lana-2-children-of-the-leaf-interview-art-direction-varied-environments-hybrid-robots-and-more); [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- Fe: the player sings to befriend young animals, which help Fe find the adult. Completing a task for the adult teaches that species' song "language," which opens new interactions. Reviewers say the pitch must be "just right." — [VideoGamer](https://videogamer.com/reviews/fe-review); [Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/fe)
- Unravel: animals help or hinder. Fish pull Yarny across water, birds help him soar and crabs chase him. The outlet was not confirmed. — [Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/unravel-review/)
- Klonoa: the Wind Bullet inflates an enemy so Klonoa can carry it, throw it, or use it to double-jump. Later stages chain grab, jump, grab, jump to reach high ledges. Klonoa 2 has color-changing enemy puzzles. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/klonoa-review-do-hew-wahoo-453115171); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/klonoa-phantasy-reverie-series-review-in-dreams-i-walk-with-you)
- Lost in Play (siblings Toto and Gal): some logic puzzles fail instantly, for example letting Toto get too close to a bear. — [Rock Paper Shotgun review mirror](https://wwwrockpapers.pages.dev/posts/lost-in-play-review-a-gorgeous-point-and-click-puzzler)
- Never Alone: spirit animals appear as platforms only when the fox is nearby. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/never-alone-review)

#### G. Wind, weather and seasons
- Never Alone: icy gusts blow at "seemingly random intervals," and the player holds a button to brace. Later the player rides gusts to cross larger gaps or reach unreachable platforms. Engadget calls riding gusts one of the game's most enjoyable mechanics. GameSpot says the gust pattern is hard to read and the controls too imprecise for late-game timing. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/never-alone-review/1900-6415968/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-12-10-never-alone-review-into-the-storm.html); [PSU](https://www.psu.com/reviews/never-alone-review-creates-a-new-genre-with-a-fascinating-insight-into-inuit-culture/)
- Seasons After Fall: switching season changes water level, ice and plant state across the whole scene (see C and E). — [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/seasons-after-fall-review/)
- Ori, Misty Woods: fog and mist hide the map, and some areas change so the player must backtrack. — [Gamer Walkthroughs](https://gamerwalkthroughs.com/ori-and-the-blind-forest/)

#### H. Sound and music
- The Witness, jungle: line puzzles match birdsong pitch, with high tweets moving the line up and low ones down. Later panels add distracting sounds, including a fake ringing phone, and a bamboo area has overlapping chirps. — [GameRevolution guide](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=12219); [Straight Dope forum thread](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/the-witness-puzzle-game/1005336?page=11)
- Planet of Lana: the composer proposed "'good' and 'bad' melodies that you can use throughout the game to solve puzzles," and robots communicate by melody. In early QA, "it was very hard for players to understand where the pitch was," so the team reduced "the noisy part of the sound." This produced a cave pipe-organ puzzle, and for the desert puzzle "we had to stay tonal." — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/planet-of-lana-game-audio/)
- Fe: song is the main verb for animals and the environment, and pitch accuracy is a frequent complaint. — [VideoGamer](https://videogamer.com/reviews/fe-review)
- Toki Tori 2: whistle "songs," meaning patterns of the basic whistle, unlock extra effects. — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/toki_tori_2)

#### I. Navigation and mazes
- Ori, Misty Woods: a mist-shrouded maze that is solved for good by restoring a lantern (see D). — [Gamer Walkthroughs](https://gamerwalkthroughs.com/ori-and-the-blind-forest/)
- I found little sourced material on 2D map-reading, compass or marked-tree trails in this pass (see Gaps).

#### J. Pattern, symbol and code puzzles placed in nature
- The Witness: line patterns are hidden in the environment, in sand markings, cloud alignments, cast shadows, pipes and tree-canopy outlines. They form "a second tier of discovery that many players overlook." — [GameRevolution guide](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=12219); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/great-moments-in-pc-gaming-spotting-your-first-environmental-puzzle-in-the-witness)
- Lost in Play: logic puzzles and minigames, nearly all tied to the story. Reviewers note "too many sliding puzzles" and praise the hint system for children and adults. — [Rock Paper Shotgun review mirror](https://wwwrockpapers.pages.dev/posts/lost-in-play-review-a-gorgeous-point-and-click-puzzler); [PlayTested](https://playtested.pages.dev/article/2023-07-16-well-crafted-puzzles-and-a-beautiful-classic-style-adventure-game-demo-review-lost-in); [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/61435/lost-in-play-switch-review)
- Lumino City, an indoor and town reference: hands-on puzzles have players "push pins in locks, rewire circuit boards, and puncture punch cards." — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/lumino-city/lumino-city-pc-review)
- Klonoa 2: color-matching enemy puzzles. — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/klonoa-phantasy-reverie-series-review-in-dreams-i-walk-with-you)

#### K. Rope, tether, swinging, climbing and lifting
- Unravel Two: two Yarnys are tied together. A Yarny can latch onto a grapple point and rappel or swing, and partners swing from each other or one anchors the other. Puzzles deliberately tangle the pair. The jump button also extends the tether, so it's easy to drop the partner by accident. — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/reviews/unravel-2-review-partners-in-twine/1900-6416931/); [Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/unravel_two); [Third Coast Review](https://thirdcoastreview.com/2018/06/18/game-review-unravel-two)
- Unravel Two, criticism: Destructoid estimated "about 70% of puzzles can be solved with one Yarny." Nintendo Life says synchronizing swings in co-op can be a challenge. Nintendo World Report, by contrast, says the game makes both players feel necessary. — [Destructoid](https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=224565); [Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/unravel_two); [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/50134)
- Brothers: the big brother boosts the little brother to high spaces (see A). — [WalkthroughKing](https://www.walkthroughking.com/text/brothers.aspx)
- Never Alone: Nuna climbs ladders and ropes, and spirit platforms and ladders appear for her (see A). — [Nintendo listing](https://nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-download-software/Never-Alone-Arctic-Collection-2172916.html)
- Max: vines to climb and swing, which also tie objects together (see E). — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/max_the_curse_of_brotherhood/b/xboxone/archive/2013/12/20/the-novelty-is-drying-out)
- Trine: the Thief's grappling rope. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/trine)

#### L. Chase, escape, stealth and timing
- Ori, Ginso Tree escape: rising water forces a long run with no checkpoints. The developers said the design needed strong readability, and acknowledged some players found the escape insurmountable. Easy Mode added checkpoints. — [Edge "The Making Of" via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20161013/283407716237477); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/OriAndTheBlindForest)
- Planet of Lana II: the player opens an underwater door without being seen by shark monsters. Playtests led the team to add tunnels around the second shark so the hiding spot (an ink-cloud placement at a tunnel intersection) felt natural. — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- Bramble: The Mountain King: there is no conventional combat, so every enemy encounter needs a puzzle-based solution, such as hiding or avoiding. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/bramble-the-mountain-king-review/); [Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/bramble-the-mountain-king-review-sinister-storybook/)
- Never Alone: wind gusts act as a timing hazard (see G). — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/never-alone-review/1900-6415968/)
- Toki Tori 2: players must do the right thing "at the exact moment." — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/toki_tori_2)

#### M. Building, repairing, tools and combinations
- Toki Tori (first game): each level gives a fixed set of tools, such as bridge builder, telewarp and freeze, and using one wrongly can make a level unbeatable. Tools are introduced gradually over 80+ levels. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Tori)
- Kirby Star Allies: "Friend Abilities" combine two allies' powers to solve puzzles. Some paths are blocked by elemental obstacles that need specific combos, for example water or ice to put out fires. — [WiKirby](https://www.wikirby.com/wiki/Friend_Action); [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/features/4-reasons-why-star-allies-isnt-just-another-kirby-game/3/)
- Max: branches become rafts and are pushed around stages, and vines tie objects together (see E). — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/max-the-curse-of-brotherhood-review/)
- Snipperclips: two players cut each other's shapes to solve physical puzzles. The developers noticed players reusing the same shapes, so later levels were designed around different shapes. Adam Vian said designing about 100 levels made him "a much better puzzle designer." Tom Vian said messing up "should often be funnier than the right solution." — [Nintendo Life feature](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/03/feature_snipperclips_-_working_with_nintendo_to_create_a_switch_co-op_classic); [Game Informer Afterwords](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2017/05/18/afterwords-snipperclips-cut-it-out-together.aspx)

#### N. Split information and communication (cross-cutting)
- We Were Here series: two players in separate spaces talk by walkie-talkie. The developers said "we intended the communication to be as hard as possible. We deliberately design puzzles to be 'extra hard' (read: fun) to describe." — [Xbox Addict interview](https://xboxaddict.com/Article/433/We-Were-Here-Forever-Interview-with-Developers-at-Total-Mayhem-Games.html)
- We Were Here: the idea came from combining a walkie-talkie concept with Myst-like puzzles. The team tested puzzles by putting people in a locked room with a walkie-talkie. — [Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-total-mayhem-games-i-we-were-here-i-)
- We Were Here: the team never used split-screen, because seeing each other's side would undermine the asymmetry. — [The Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/?p=43661)
- We Were Here: designers ask what each puzzle will make players explain. "In a lot of games, that's sort of bad puzzle design, but for us it's half the fun." — [Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/news/we-were-here-forever-turns-brave-adventurers-into-voices-in-the-dark)

#### O. A third "manipulator" role (reference for designing support roles)
- Rayman Legends: Murfy appears in about half the levels, and 14 levels are Murfy-essential and built to be solo-friendly. Reviewers were split, with one saying Murfy "adds a minor but noticeable layer of challenge" at best and is "an unnecessary complication" at worst. — [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayman_Legends); [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/games/rayman_legends/b/wii_u/archive/2013/09/03/rayman-hops-from-strength-to-strength.aspx)
- Child of Light: the firefly helper role tapers off over the game (see D). — [Co-Optimus review](https://co-optimus.com/review/1365/page/2/child-of-light-co-op-review.html)

### Inferences
Below is one design card per family, adapted to the sister (S, tall, reaches high, heavier) and the brother (B, small, crawls, fits gaps, lighter). Netcode tiers are defined in section 5: Tier 1 is discrete state and easy, Tier 2 is owned or scripted motion and medium, and Tier 3 is free physics or tight timing and hard.

**0. Reach and access (tall/small): the core verb split**
- How it works: S reaches high hooks, shelves, branches and knotholes and can boost or lift B. B crawls through roots, burrows, hollow logs, fence gaps and bars. This mirrors Brothers (bars, boost, carry), Never Alone (gaps versus heavy objects) and Thomas Was Alone (gap versus height).
- Forest flavor: B crawls into the old oak's hollow and comes out at a high knothole. S pulls down a low branch so B can climb it. B squeezes under a fallen log to reach a lever S can't.
- Indoor/town flavor: dog flaps, vents, under-bed spaces and banisters for B. Top shelves, wall hooks and high latches for S.
- Simple version: B opens a gate from the far side (Brothers gate crank). Advanced version: chains of give-and-take where each sibling's access unlocks the other's, ending with both needed at the same moment through a hold-and-go action.
- Tier 1.

**1. Weight and balance (see-saw, pulley, counterweight, scales, pressure plates, carrying, stacking)**
- How it works: discrete weights on discrete supports. Sister counts as heavy, brother as light, and objects such as rocks, logs, pinecone baskets and acorn sacks have set weights.
- Two-player split: B alone can't lower a see-saw that S can, and S can't stand on a thin branch that holds B. Pulley lifts: S stands in the counterweight bucket to raise B, and B rides up with a rock to lower S. Scales: the weight on two pans opens a gate only when balanced, and the siblings' known weights become the "units." It Takes Two's sap-weighted wheel is a good model for a "weigh one side" puzzle.
- Forest flavor: a log see-saw over a stream, a well bucket on a rope, a beaver-dam sluice that needs weight on a lever, a bird-feeder pulley, stepping stones that sink under S but not B.
- Simple version: one pressure plate holds a door open while the other walks through. Hold buttons already exist in the game, and this matches Brothers' hold-the-lever cage.
- Advanced version: a three-stage pulley lift where S must leave the counterweight at the right moment. Or scales that need the exact item combination, so players must reason about weights.
- Indoor/town: dumbwaiters, elevator counterweights, laundry chutes, coat-rack see-saws.
- Readability: show tilt angles in visibly discrete steps (for example 3 positions), and draw weights as clearly sized objects.
- Tier 1 with discrete states. Tier 3 if built as free rigid-body physics.

**2. Water (levels, streams, dams, waterwheels, floating objects, waterfalls hiding passages, rafts)**
- How it works: the water level is a single integer per basin, for example levels 0 to 3. Sluice gates, dams and plugs move water between basins, and floating objects rise and fall with the level. A waterfall is a curtain that hides a cave and can be parted by redirecting flow upstream.
- Two-player split: B can't swim, so S carries B through deep water (Brothers) or B rides a floating crate, nutshell boat or lily pad. S works a high sluice wheel while B, down in the dry riverbed, collects or plugs something before the water returns. One sibling sees the pond's level gauge (split information) while the other works the dam.
- Forest flavor: the waterfall curtain hides a grotto, and diverting the stream with a log or rock dam thins the curtain. A waterwheel powers a lift or log flume. A raft is built from logs and vine rope (family 12). In a rain-filled pond (family 6), the brother floats up on a crate as water rises.
- Simple version: one lever drains a pool one step to reveal a path, with a visible line on the rock showing where the water will stop.
- Advanced version: two linked basins where raising one lowers the other, so the siblings must plan which side each needs. Or a combination like Seasons After Fall: raise the water, then freeze or float to reach height.
- Avoid the Water Temple trap: let players change water where they stand, or show all target heights, to avoid backtracking (Goomba Stomp critique).
- Finale: a slow rising-water escape (Ori Ginso style) with frequent checkpoints, given the reported frustration with the Ginso escape.
- Indoor/town: bathtub, sink, flooded basement, fountain, canal lock.
- Tier 1 for stepped water levels and floats moving with the level. Tier 3 for fluid simulation or free-floating physics rafts.

**3. Light and shadow (sunbeams, mirrors, fireflies, lanterns, shadow casting, day/night)**
- How it works: beams travel in a few fixed directions and bounce off mirrors or shiny objects (a dewdrop, a puddle, a metal pan) set to discrete angles, and they light receivers such as flowers that open in sun or crystals. Portable lights (a firefly jar, a lantern) reveal hidden paths or calm night creatures. Shadows act as solid or safe zones.
- Two-player split: S holds a mirror or lantern high, since only she reaches the sunbeam, while B carries a firefly jar through dark roots. One player positions the light and the other walks the shadow path, as in Projection and Murfy. One sibling sees shadow-revealed symbols that only appear from their side.
- Forest flavor: canopy gaps let in sunbeams, and moving a branch aside lets light reach a sleeping flower. Fireflies follow a lantern. Dusk and dawn change which paths are lit. Moonflowers open only at night.
- Simple version: open one shutter or branch so the sunbeam hits one flower, which opens a petal bridge.
- Advanced version: chain two or three mirrors held by both siblings to hit two receivers at once (Fireboy prism levels), or a day/night toggle that changes which creatures and paths exist.
- Indoor/town: window blinds, a flashlight, a projector, streetlights, puppet shadows on a wall.
- Readability: beams should be bright, chunky lines with a clear glow on the target, and mirrors should snap to visible angles.
- Tier 1 with snapped angles and ray casting from synced state. Projection-style continuous shadow platforms are Tier 2 or 3 and were called "finicky."

**4. Plants and growth (vines, seeds, sun flowers, bounce mushrooms, logs as bridges, tree hollows)**
- How it works: plants have growth stages (seed, sprout, vine) triggered by water, sun or song. Mushrooms bounce, and they can stand in for a jump verb if jumping stays out. Fallen logs are pushable bridges, and hollows are crawlspaces.
- Two-player split: B plants a seed in a low burrow while S waters it from a high ledge or redirects sunlight to it. S pushes a log over a gap and B crawls inside it as a tunnel. Max's pillar, branch and vine combinations suggest pairing growth with other systems.
- Forest flavor: vines grow up the oak when a sunbeam hits them. Sunflowers turn to face the light (a dial puzzle). Acorns planted in a ring of stones grow a ladder. Puffball mushrooms bounce B but not S, since S is too heavy, which gives a split.
- Simple version: water one seed so a vine ladder grows.
- Advanced version: grow in the right order (Seasons After Fall's double back), or plants that block as well as help, such as brambles that must be wilted by shade.
- Indoor/town: potted plants, a greenhouse, ivy on walls, window boxes.
- Readability: every growth stage needs a distinct silhouette, and seeds sparkle where they can be planted.
- Tier 1, since growth is discrete states.

**5. Animals (follow, lure, calm, ride, herd, avoid)**
- How it works: animals run simple state machines (idle, attracted, scared, eating, sleeping) that respond to the siblings' actions. This is the Toki Tori 2 whistle and stomp model, where call means come and stomp means flee. Planet of Lana II goes further and lets the player control the animal.
- Two-player split: S reaches the berries on a high branch and drops them, and B, low and quiet, lures the frog or squirrel to a spot. One sibling scares the deer herd one way while the other waits to cross. B is small enough to ride a goose, dog or deer and S is not. Calming a startled animal needs one sibling to hum a tune while the other moves past.
- Forest flavor: squirrels carry acorns or keys up the oak, a heron blocks the stream until distracted with a fish, bees are calmed by smoke or a flower, frogs act as moving lily-pad platforms, fish ferry B (Unravel fish), and an owl gives night-only help.
- Simple version: drop a berry so a bird moves off a branch.
- Advanced version: chains like the Toki Tori 2 berry-and-frog example: stomp drops food, the animal walks to a plate, the plate opens a door. Or herding two animals into separate pens at once.
- Family tone: animals should be helpers or gentle obstacles. Lost in Play's bear shows how instant failure annoyed one reviewer, so use soft resets.
- Indoor/town: a cat, pigeons, a dog to lure with a ball, goldfish.
- Tier 2, because animals move. Keep their AI deterministic and host-run, and sync state and target rather than free movement.

**6. Wind, weather and seasons**
- How it works: environmental states such as wind direction, rain or dry, fog or clear, season, and frozen or thawed change what the world allows.
- Two-player split: one sibling uses the weather vane or season shrine and the other uses the result (Seasons After Fall in two-player form). Wind pushes light B but not heavy S, so S must anchor B by holding hands (Never Alone bracing). Fog hides the path for the walker while the sibling on a high lookout calls directions (split information).
- Forest flavor: wind turns windmill seeds or a pinwheel lift, a rain cloud fills the pond (family 2), frost makes the stream walkable, and fog lifts when a bell rings.
- Simple version: one toggle (sunny or rainy) changes pond height.
- Advanced version: chained states (rain, then freeze, then walk) or cyclic wind that both siblings must time crossings to.
- Readability: telegraph wind clearly and early. GameSpot and an itch.io player both said Never Alone's gust cue was hard to read or arrived late.
- Indoor/town: fans, AC vents, a sprinkler system, a freezer.
- Tier 1 if weather is a toggled state. Periodic wind is Tier 2 when driven by a shared server timer.

**7. Sound and music (echo, bird call sequences, chimes, rhythm)**
- How it works: repeat a heard sequence (Simon-style), match pitch (Witness birds, Fe), use good and bad melodies as keys (Planet of Lana), or use echoes to locate a hidden cave.
- Two-player split: one sibling hears or sees the bird's call pattern and the other plays it on chimes, flowers or stones out of earshot (split information). S reaches high bells while B hits low ones, so a melody needs both.
- Forest flavor: wind chimes in the oak, a woodpecker rhythm, frogs croaking in pitch order, stones that ring by size.
- Simple version: a three-note call and response with visual notes (colors and heights) plus sound.
- Advanced version: overlapping calls (Witness bamboo), a melody split across the two siblings, or rhythm doors.
- Accessibility: Planet of Lana found players struggled to locate pitch. Always pair each note with a visual cue (color, height, shape), and keep sounds tonal and clean.
- Indoor/town: piano, doorbells, music box, pipes.
- Tier 1, since note order is discrete. Avoid needing both players to press on the beat at the same time.

**8. Navigation and mazes (loops, landmarks, map reading, compass, signs, marked trees)**
- How it works: a forest loop resets unless the right landmark sequence is followed. The map, signs or carved marks tell the way, and lighting a lantern can "straighten" the maze (Ori Misty Woods).
- Two-player split: the treasure map in the story is natural split information. One sibling holds the map page and the other sees the trail. S climbs a lookout to see the layout while B, at ground level, reads markers on roots. Only B finds marks under mushrooms, and only S sees marks high on trunks.
- Simple version: follow arrows of three marked trees.
- Advanced version: the map uses symbols (a mushroom means left, a feather means up) that the siblings must decode together, or the forest rearranges until a landmark is lit.
- Readability: in side view, loops need clear "you've been here" cues, such as a distinctive stump or a bird that follows.
- Indoor/town: a city signpost puzzle, a subway map, a house blueprint.
- Tier 1.

**9. Pattern, symbol and code puzzles in nature (carvings, stones, constellations, cairns)**
- How it works: symbols carved in bark, stones stacked in a cairn order, a constellation seen through a canopy gap, leaf colors as a code.
- Two-player split: the code is on one side of a wall or river and the input on the other (We Were Here style), or half the symbols are high (S) and half are low or hidden in roots (B).
- Forest flavor: owl, fox and bear carvings around the oak give an order. A cairn stack must match a painting on a cave wall behind the waterfall. Shadow patterns appear only at a set time (Witness Shady Trees).
- Simple version: match three symbols to three stones.
- Advanced version: symbols that combine (two carvings make one meaning), or codes hidden in the environment (Witness-like second tier) as optional collectibles.
- Caution: Lost in Play reviewers disliked repeated sliding puzzles, so vary formats. Keep hints available, since Lost in Play's hint system was praised for families.
- Indoor/town: combination locks, keypads, posters, clock hands (Lumino City-style pins and circuits).
- Tier 1, and the best fit for low-latency-sensitive online play.

**10. Rope, tether, swinging, climbing, ladders, lifting**
- How it works: ropes are tied between anchors as bridges (Unravel knots). A partner acts as an anchor (Unravel Two). Characters climb vines and ladders. A boost or lift is S raising B (Brothers).
- Two-player split: S lowers a rope from a ledge and B climbs, or B ties a rope at a low post under a root and S crosses. Boost is the cheapest high-value co-op verb and needs no physics.
- Simple version: boost B onto a ledge, and B drops a ladder for S.
- Advanced version: a pulley lift made with the rope (family 1), a vine bridge that holds only B, or a swing sequence.
- Caution: Unravel Two reviewers noted accidental partner drops and hard-to-sync swings, which become worse online.
- Indoor/town: bedsheet rope, drainpipes, fire escapes, step-stools.
- Tier 1 for boost, ladder and climb with snapped positions. Tier 3 for a free-physics tether or swing.

**11. Chase, escape, stealth and timing**
- How it works: something pursues the siblings (rising water, a grumpy boar, a swarm of bees), or a watcher patrols (a sleepy bear, an owl). The players hide in shadows or bushes or behind the waterfall.
- Two-player split: one sibling distracts while the other sneaks (a lure as in family 5). One holds the hiding-spot cover, for example a big leaf, for both. B hides where S can't (Planet of Lana II's ink-cloud hiding spot, adapted).
- Simple version: a sleeping animal wakes only on noise, so walk slowly past.
- Advanced version: a scripted, slow, forgiving chase finale, such as a waterfall flood or a stampede, with checkpoints every screen.
- Family tone: Ori's Ginso escape was reported as punishing, so keep chases short, scripted and checkpointed.
- Indoor/town: sneaking past a sleeping parent or a neighbor's dog.
- Tier 2 for scripted chasers. Tier 3 if success depends on precise simultaneous timing between two remote players.

**12. Building and repairing (fix a bridge, assemble a raft, gather parts)**
- How it works: gather N parts and place them in slots (Toki Tori's bridge builder as a tool, Max's branch rafts), with each part findable by only one sibling.
- Two-player split: B fetches the planks from under the roots, S fetches the rope from high branches, and both assemble at a slot. Repair steps can need both: one holds the plank while the other ties it, as a hold-and-act action.
- Forest flavor: a broken rope bridge over the waterfall gorge, a raft from logs and vines, a squirrel's broken pulley, a beaver lodge.
- Simple version: one missing plank.
- Advanced version: a multi-part build where parts come from other puzzle families, such as a log freed from the dam (water) or vine rope grown by sun (plants). This makes the build a level finale.
- Indoor/town: fix a toy, rewire a lamp, assemble a cart.
- Tier 1, since slots fill as discrete events.

**13. Split information and communication (cross-cutting layer)**
- How it works: each player sees or knows something the other needs. The design goal, as We Were Here puts it, is puzzles that are fun to describe.
- Fit: the treasure map is ideal. Only one sibling sees a map fragment, carving or sound, and the other acts on it. This works with families 3, 7, 8 and 9.
- Simple version: one sibling reads a symbol aloud and the other picks it.
- Advanced version: both hold half of a code, as in a map torn in two.
- Online note: this works without voice chat if both have a simple ping or emote system. That is an inference, and we should test whether family players will use voice.
- Tier 1.

**14. Companion and helper control (optional extension)**
- Planet of Lana II's "become the animal" suggests B could temporarily ride or guide a squirrel or frog to reach new places, giving a third ability source without new sibling verbs. Toki Tori 2's two-verb model, call and scare, is a cheap way to make animals puzzle pieces.
- Tier 2.

Only the Planet of Lana II Xbox Wire article and the A Sound Effect Planet of Lana audio interview were read in full.

## 2. What makes each family readable at a glance in 2D side view?

### Takeaway
Developers repeatedly stress readability. Planet of Lana's team checks whether the goal is clear, whether failure gives feedback, and whether elements appear in the right order. Ori's devs said the escape design needed strong readability. Toki Tori 2 teaches everything through visual cues with no UI. Readability problems in reviews come from imprecise or continuous inputs (Max's drawing, Projection's shadows), late-telegraphed hazards (Never Alone's wind), audio-only cues (Planet of Lana pitch, Fe singing) and state that is hard to see globally (the Water Temple).

### Cited Findings
- Planet of Lana II's sketch-review questions include "Is the goal for the player clear enough?", whether failure gives players "the feedback they need in order to not get stuck," and "Do we present the elements of the puzzle in the right order?" Art, lighting and visual detail are added only after a puzzle tests well. — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- Planet of Lana II: tunnels were added after playtests so the hiding spot "felt more natural" to players. — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- Ori: the developers said the design needed strong readability, and some players found the Ginso escape "insurmountable." — [Edge via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20161013/283407716237477)
- Toki Tori 2: "no words, no tutorial and no UI overlay… everything the player needs to know is taught through visual cues and curiosity." — [Snackbar Games](https://www.snackbar-games.com/reviews/toki-tori-2-a-minimalist-puzzling-masterpiece); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/toki_tori_2)

### Inferences
These are readability cues per family for a warm storybook side view:
- **Reach/access:** a consistent visual language. B-only gaps share one look (for example a dark arch of roots with a small glow), and S-only reach points share another (a hook or ribbon tied high). Fireboy-style color coding can tint interactables subtly with each sibling's color.
- **Weight/balance:** discrete tilt poses, obvious weight silhouettes (S visibly heavier, rocks chunky), and a needle or bobber that shows balance.
- **Water:** draw the target water lines on rocks as tide marks before any change, show each basin's level as a gauge (a stick with notches), and show floating objects with visible ripples.
- **Light:** thick glowing beams, mirrors that snap with a click, receivers that visibly "drink" light (a flower opening in stages).
- **Plants:** distinct growth-stage silhouettes, and sparkle or soil patches where seeds go (Max's glowing marked spots).
- **Animals:** exaggerated idle, attracted and scared poses, a thought bubble showing what the animal wants (a berry icon), and gaze lines.
- **Wind/weather:** telegraph before effect, with leaves blowing, grass leaning and a sound swell well before gusts. Never Alone shows what happens when cues are late.
- **Sound:** every note paired with a color, height and shape, clean tonal sounds, and a replay button.
- **Navigation:** unique landmarks per screen and a "you looped" cue such as the same cheeky bird.
- **Symbols:** large, few, distinct symbols, and repeat each symbol in the input location.
- **Rope/climb:** snapped climb points with a hand icon on hover or approach.
- **Chase:** pursuer always visible on screen, clear safe-zone shading.
- **Build:** a ghost outline of the missing part in its slot.
- Prefer discrete, snapped interactions over freeform input (drawing, analog aiming), since the readability complaints in sources cluster around continuous controls.

- No source quantified readability, such as playtest success rates by cue type.
- No 2D-specific art-direction source on readability for nature puzzles (for example foreground and background separation in foliage-heavy scenes) was found in this pass.

## 3. How can each family be split so one player's ability or knowledge is needed by the other?

### Takeaway
Two kinds of split recur. Ability splits are reach and fit, strength and weight, swim and float, and tools (Brothers, Never Alone, Thomas Was Alone, Trine, It Takes Two). Information splits mean each player knows something the other needs (We Were Here). Research by Harris (CHI 2019) found that designed asymmetry and interdependence can raise connectedness, engagement and immersion, especially when the timing and direction of dependence are tuned. Reviews warn about co-op that is optional or solvable solo (Unravel Two's estimated 70%, Little Nightmares III's optional co-op), and about support roles that fade (Child of Light, Murfy, Botanicula's underused abilities).

### Cited Findings
- Asymmetric cooperative games may raise players' sense of connectedness, engagement and immersion compared with symmetric ones. Tuning the timing and direction of dependence improved these further. — [University of Waterloo Touch Lab, Harris CHI 2019](https://uwaterloo.ca/touchlab/references/asymmetry-and-beyond-improving-social-connectedness)
- Harris's thesis names directional dependence, synchronicity, necessity, leadership and primacy as dynamics of asymmetric cooperative play. — [UWSpace thesis record](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/handle/10012/14710?show=full)
- Unravel Two: about 70% of puzzles are solvable with one Yarny, so co-op felt "bolted on" (Destructoid). Nintendo World Report felt the opposite. — [Destructoid](https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=224565); [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/50134)
- Little Nightmares III: one reviewer said making co-op optional (an AI partner in solo) limited what designers could do with puzzles. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/platformer/little-nightmares-3-review); [FingerGuns](https://fingerguns.net/reviews/2025/10/14/little-nightmares-iii-review-ps5-wake-me-up-when-its-all-over/)
- Never Alone: spirits only appear when the fox is nearby, so the girl's path depends on the fox's position. Reviewers said it works best with two players. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/never-alone-review); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/never_alone)
- Brothers: hold-to-keep-open interactions, where the gate falls if the other stops turning, create interdependence (see section 1A). — [Ludo.guide remake part 6](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-remake-part-6)
- It Takes Two: tools must be combined, with sap to weigh and matches to ignite, so neither player can progress alone. — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2025/04/08/spencer-mda-8-kinds-of-fun/); [Gamepur](https://www.gamepur.com/?p=152294)
- We Were Here: separated players and no split-screen, with puzzles designed to be "extra hard" to describe. — [Xbox Addict](https://xboxaddict.com/Article/433/We-Were-Here-Forever-Interview-with-Developers-at-Total-Mayhem-Games.html); [The Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/?p=43661)
- Child of Light: the second player's puzzle role "tapers off severely." — [Co-Optimus review](https://co-optimus.com/review/1365/page/2/child-of-light-co-op-review.html)
- Rayman Legends: Murfy-essential levels were made solo-friendly, and reviews were split on whether Murfy added much. — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/games/rayman_legends/b/wii_u/archive/2013/09/03/rayman-hops-from-strength-to-strength.aspx); [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayman_Legends)
- Botanicula: unique hero abilities are used "only sporadically," which reviewers called a missed opportunity. — [OutNow](https://outnow.ch/en/Games/2012/Botanicula/Review/)
- Snipperclips: players reused the same solutions, so designers built later levels around different shapes. — [Nintendo Life feature](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/03/feature_snipperclips_-_working_with_nintendo_to_create_a_switch_co-op_classic)

### Inferences
These are split patterns to reuse across all families.
1. **Reach versus fit (ability).** S does anything high, and B does anything low, narrow or hidden. This is the default and already in the game.
2. **Heavy versus light (ability).** S presses plates, sinks see-saws and anchors against wind. B rides floats, crosses thin branches and bounces on mushrooms. Weight can be a passive trait with no new button.
3. **Hold-and-go (timing without sync risk).** One holds a lever, plate, branch or rope while the other moves. This builds on the existing press/hold verb, comes straight from Brothers, and avoids simultaneous-press timing.
4. **Give-and-take chains (directional dependence).** Alternate who helps whom. B opens a gate for S, S boosts B, B drops a ladder. Harris's work suggests tuning the direction and timing of dependence matters, so avoid one sibling always serving the other. The Child of Light and Murfy fade is a warning.
5. **See versus do (information split).** One sibling sees the clue (a map fragment, a carving high up, a bird pattern only audible on their ledge) and the other operates the input. The treasure map is the natural carrier.
6. **Two halves (combination).** Each sibling holds half a key item (two map halves, two parts of a melody, two weights that balance). This follows It Takes Two's combining tools.
7. **Separation set pieces.** Briefly split the siblings, one above and one below the waterfall, to force information sharing (We Were Here's no-shared-view principle).

Avoiding "solo-solvable" puzzles:
- Every puzzle should need at least one S-only and one B-only action, which is the inverse of the Unravel Two 70% problem.
- If an AI or solo mode is ever added, expect design constraints like those reported for Little Nightmares III.
- Give both siblings rising, varied roles across the chapter so neither becomes a "button-holder" support role (the Botanicula, Child of Light and Murfy lesson).

Per-family split summary:

| Family | Sister (tall/heavy) | Brother (small/light) | Info split option |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight/balance | Counterweight, presses heavy plate | Rides lift, crosses thin branch | One reads the scale, the other loads it |
| Water | Turns high sluice wheel, carries B in deep water | Floats on crate or leaf, plugs low drain | One sees the level gauge, the other works the dam |
| Light | Holds mirror up in sunbeam | Carries firefly jar through roots | Shadow symbol visible only from one side |
| Plants | Waters or lights from above | Plants seed in burrow, crawls log | One knows which seed, the other where |
| Animals | Drops food from high branch | Lures quietly, rides small animal | One hears the animal's call pattern |
| Wind/weather | Anchors against wind | Light enough to be carried by updraft | Lookout calls directions through fog |
| Sound | Rings high chimes | Strikes low stones | One hears the bird, the other plays it |
| Navigation | Sees layout from lookout | Reads root-level trail marks | Map half each |
| Symbols | High carvings | Low or hidden carvings | Code on one side, lock on the other |
| Rope/climb | Boosts, lowers rope | Climbs, ties rope at low post | — |
| Chase/stealth | Holds cover or distracts | Hides in small spaces, sneaks | One watches the patrol, calls "go" |
| Build | Fetches high parts, holds plank | Fetches low parts, ties knot | One has the blueprint |

### Gaps
- Harris's research used a lab prototype, not a commercial family game, and I did not read the full paper for effect sizes.
- No source covered family or child-specific co-op dynamics, such as parent and child playing together online.

## 4. What is a simple first version and an advanced version for each family?

### Takeaway
Sources favor introducing one element at a time, in context, then combining. Planet of Lana tests puzzles inside the wider game because isolated testing can make a puzzle "much more difficult than intended" if too many elements appear at once. Toki Tori introduces tools gradually over 80+ levels, Seasons After Fall builds puzzles that make players double back after a state change, and Klonoa chains its single mechanic into longer sequences. Escalation through repetition alone draws criticism (Water Temple, Botanicula, Lost in Play sliding puzzles). Snipperclips shows escalation by forcing new solutions.

### Cited Findings
- Planet of Lana II: puzzles build on earlier elements, so testing in isolation could make a puzzle "much more difficult than intended" if too many new elements appear at once. Not progressing immediately is fine if players stay engaged, but "a fine line between a fun challenge and a frustrating experience." The sequel aimed for "deeper and more elaborate mechanics" while keeping the flow and accessibility of the first game. — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- Toki Tori: tools are introduced gradually across 80+ levels and five worlds. Each level has a fixed tool set. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Tori)
- Toki Tori 2: every puzzle can be solved with the basic whistle and stomp, and later songs add specialist patterns. — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/toki_tori_2); [Snackbar Games](https://www.snackbar-games.com/reviews/toki-tori-2-a-minimalist-puzzling-masterpiece)
- Seasons After Fall: interact in one season, then double back. Raise water, then freeze it, as a combined puzzle. — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=276105); [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/seasons-after-fall-review/)
- Klonoa: later stages chain enemy grab and double jump repeatedly to reach high ledges. — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/klonoa-phantasy-reverie-series-review-in-dreams-i-walk-with-you)
- Max: puzzles escalate by combining powers, such as a vine on a pillar or branch to make a bridge. — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/max_the_curse_of_brotherhood/b/xboxone/archive/2013/12/20/the-novelty-is-drying-out)
- The Witness jungle: later sound panels add distractor noises. — [GameRevolution guide](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=12219)
- Fireboy and Watergirl 2: prism levels require two beams on target simultaneously (unofficial source). — [unofficial tips page](https://fireboyandwatergirl-eip.pages.dev/blog/fireboy-watergirl-2-light-temple-tips-mastering-mirror-puzzles)
- Never Alone: wind starts as a hazard to brace against and later becomes a tool to ride across gaps. — [PSU](https://www.psu.com/reviews/never-alone-review-creates-a-new-genre-with-a-fascinating-insight-into-inuit-culture/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-12-10-never-alone-review-into-the-storm.html)
- Water Temple: criticized for "exhausting repetition" when escalation means re-cycling the same water states. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Temple_(Ocarina_of_Time))
- Snipperclips: designers watched players and built new levels requiring different shapes. Messing up should be funny. — [Nintendo Life feature](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/03/feature_snipperclips_-_working_with_nintendo_to_create_a_switch_co-op_classic)

### Inferences
A useful escalation template for one forest chapter is teach, then twist, then combine, then finale. The Never Alone wind arc (hazard to tool) is a good model for the twist step.

| Family | First version (teach) | Twist | Advanced (combine) | Finale use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weight/balance | Hold plate, other walks through | Plate needs S, B too light | Pulley lift where both swap roles mid-way | Scales that must balance with items from other puzzles |
| Water | One lever drains a pool one step | Floating crate rises with water and carries B | Two linked basins, plus waterfall diverted to reveal grotto | Slow rising-water escape with checkpoints |
| Light | Move a branch so sunbeam hits a flower | Mirror (a dewdrop or shiny pan) redirects the beam | Two-mirror chain held by both siblings, hitting two targets | Night section lit only by firefly jar and lantern |
| Plants | Water a seed so a vine ladder grows | Vine grows only in sunlight (link to light) | Grow in order, wilting brambles with shade | Old oak "wakes," branches become stairs |
| Animals | Drop berry so bird moves | Lure frog onto lily-pad switch | Chain (stomp drops food, animal walks, plate opens) | Ride a friendly deer or goose across the river |
| Wind/weather | Gust pushes leaf boat | Wind blows B but not S, so hold hands | Rain fills pond, freezing makes a path | Fog lifted by ringing a bell |
| Sound | 3-note bird call and response | Notes split high (S) and low (B) | Overlapping calls with distractors | Song opens the waterfall door |

| Symbols | Match three carvings | Code on other side of river | Symbols combine | Cairn order shown in a painting behind the waterfall |
| Rope/climb | Boost B to ledge, B drops ladder | Rope bridge holds only B | Rope plus pulley lift | Climb the old oak together |
| Chase/stealth | Tiptoe past sleeping bear | One distracts, one sneaks | Patrol plus hiding spots plus lure | Short, scripted, checkpointed flood or stampede |

- Pacing: introduce at most one new element per puzzle, as Planet of Lana advises, and test puzzles in chapter order.
- Retire or transform mechanics rather than repeat them (the Water Temple and Botanicula critiques).
- Optional "second tier" secrets (Witness-style hidden symbols in tree shadows) can reward older players without blocking younger ones.

## 5. Which mechanics are easy to build and keep in sync online, and which are hard?

### Takeaway
Discrete, state-based mechanics are the easiest to network. Switches, levers, held buttons, stepped water levels, plant growth stages, snapped mirrors, symbol and code locks, sound sequences, and slot-based building all reduce to small state changes that a host or server can broadcast reliably. Free physics (stacking, ropes, swinging, floating rafts) and tight simultaneous timing across two remote players are hard. Networking references explain that deterministic lockstep sends only inputs but needs strict determinism and waits for the most lagged player, while state synchronization tolerates divergence but is "approximate and lossy." Reviews of physics-heavy co-op (Unravel Two swings, Projection shadows, Never Alone timing) report sync and precision friction even locally.

### Cited Findings
- Deterministic lockstep networks a system "by sending only the inputs that control that system, rather than the state." Floating-point determinism across platforms is hard, and players end up waiting for the most lagged player. — [Gaffer On Games, Networked Physics](https://gafferongames.com/categories/networked-physics/)
- State synchronization sends "both input and state," doesn't need perfect determinism, and is "an approximate and lossy synchronization strategy." — [Gaffer On Games, State Synchronization](https://gafferongames.com/post/state_synchronization/)
- Commercial deterministic physics toolkits, such as Photon TrueSync for Unity, require following rules on random numbers and floats, and roll back and resimulate when guessed inputs are wrong. — [Photon TrueSync docs](https://doc.photonengine.com/en-us/truesync)
- A write-up of Teardown's multiplayer describes a hybrid: destruction as a stream of deterministic commands, with object transforms and velocities state-synced. This is a secondary source of lower reliability. — [Fooqux](https://fooqux.com/article/3560)
- Academic work proposes deterministic lockstep with snapshot interpolation for resync and hot-joins. — [Deterministic Lockstep paper (BSZ)](https://hdms.bsz-bw.de/files/7107/DeterministicLockstepInNetworkedGamesPaper.pdf)
- Unravel Two: synchronizing swings in co-op "can be a challenge," and the tether can drop a partner by accident, even in local co-op. — [Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/unravel_two); [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/reviews/unravel-2-review-partners-in-twine/1900-6416931/)
- Projection: First Light: shadows are finicky in timed puzzles. — [Hooked Gamers](https://hookedgamers.com/pc/projection_first_light/review/article-2213.html)
- Never Alone: controls are too imprecise for the precise timing needed late-game. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/never-alone-review/1900-6415968/)
- Little Nightmares III ships co-op online-only, which shows online co-op puzzle platforming is viable commercially. — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/little-nightmares-iii)
- Pico Park: when one player trips, everyone restarts, which shows how physical coordination failures cascade in co-op. — [Daily Campus](https://dailycampus.com/2023/11/14/the-backlog-destroy-your-friendships-with-pico-park/)

### Inferences
These are netcode difficulty tiers for a browser game. They are my assessment, not sourced benchmarks.

**Tier 1: easy (discrete state, event-driven, latency-tolerant)**
- Levers, latching switches, hold buttons, doors, gates.
- Stepped water levels and objects that ride the level, such as a crate snapped to the water surface.
- Plant growth stages, flower open and closed, day/night or season toggles.
- Mirrors with snapped angles and beams recomputed locally from synced state.
- Symbol, code, cairn and map puzzles, and sound sequences (sync the note order, not the audio timing).
- Slot-based building and repair, and inventory pickups.
- Boost, ladders and climb points with snapped positions (play the animation locally and sync the start and end state).
- Pressure plates and see-saws with discrete tilt positions.
- Pattern: host-authoritative or server-authoritative state machine. Each interaction sends an intent ("pull lever 3"), and the authority validates it and broadcasts the new state. Late joiners get a full snapshot. This fits the existing walk, stairs, crawl and press/hold verbs.

**Tier 2: medium (moving things with clear ownership or scripted paths)**
- Carried or pushed objects (rock, crate, plank). Give the carrier ownership, sync position at a modest rate, and snap on release.
- Animals with deterministic, host-run AI states and target points, synced as state plus target, not raw motion.
- Scripted chasers (rising water, stampede) driven by a shared timeline, with each client interpolating.
- Periodic wind or moving platforms on a shared clock.
- Hold-and-go co-op timing, where one holds and the other moves, which tolerates latency because the hold is continuous.

**Tier 3: hard (avoid or heavily simplify for online browser play)**
- Free rigid-body stacking, tumbling logs, physics see-saws and physics rafts.
- Rope and tether physics, swinging, and the partner-as-anchor mechanic from Unravel Two.
- Freeform drawn powers (Max) and continuous analog light or shadow aiming (Projection).
- Puzzles that need both remote players to act within a small time window ("press together"), or fast-reaction chases with instant failure.
- Mitigations: replace physics with scripted animations between snapped states, for example a log bridge that "falls into place" when pushed past a point. Use "both hold" instead of "both press at once." Keep chase windows generous and checkpoint often.

**Recommended mix for the forest chapter**
- Build the forest's core puzzles from Tier 1 families: water levels, light and mirrors, plant growth, symbols and map, sound sequences, weight via discrete plates, scales and pulleys, and slot building.
- Add Tier 2 for life and motion: animals, carried items, a scripted waterfall or flood escape.
- Use Tier 3 only as non-blocking flavor, such as bouncy mushrooms with a canned arc animation or swaying vines that are cosmetic.

### Gaps
- No source was found that benchmarks latency tolerance for co-op puzzle interactions, for example acceptable delay for "hold" versus "press together."
- No source was found on how specific co-op puzzle platformers (Unravel Two, It Takes Two, Little Nightmares III) implement their netcode.
- Browser-specific networking (WebSocket or WebRTC latency) was outside this pass, and the Teardown hybrid source is a secondary write-up.
