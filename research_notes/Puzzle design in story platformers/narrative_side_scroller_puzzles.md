# Puzzle design in single-player, story-driven side-scrolling puzzle adventures

Scope note: these notes cover Lost in Play (2022), Planet of Lana (2023) and Planet of Lana II (2026), Limbo (2010) and Inside (2016), Little Nightmares II (2021), Amanita Design (Machinarium 2009, Botanicula 2012, Samorost 3 2016, Creaks 2020), The Gardens Between (2018), Bramble: The Mountain King (2023) and Max: The Curse of Brotherhood (2013). Games on the "if time allows" list (Gris, Toem, Chicory, Lumino City, Hob, Oddmar, Ori, Teslagrad, Fez, Braid) and Little Nightmares I were not researched. See Gaps. Several primary pages (Rock Paper Shotgun, Destructoid, GameSpot, Czech Radio, Epic Games Store) blocked fetching, so some points rely on search-result snippets. Those points are marked as such.

Release-date check: Planet of Lana II: Children of the Leaf released 5 March 2026 on PC, Xbox, PlayStation 4/5 and Switch/Switch 2 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_of_Lana_II); [Thunderful](https://thunderfulgames.com/planet-of-lana-ii-officially-releases-on-all-platforms-on-march-5-2026/)). One search snippet said the sequel came out "in 2025". That conflicts with both sources above and is most likely wrong.

## Q1. How are puzzles made to feel like part of the place and the plot (diegetic puzzles, real-world logic)?

### Takeaway
The strongest examples get puzzles out of the objects and creatures that belong to each place, and out of the action the plot already needs. Inside has you steal the submarine instead of being handed it. Gardens Between builds each island's puzzle from the objects of one shared memory. Lost in Play built its story backward from the places the artists wanted to draw. The usual way this goes wrong is abstract "puzzle furniture" such as pressure plates and convenient crates, plus filler puzzles that repeat a mechanic without moving the story.

### Cited Findings

**Lost in Play (Happy Juice Games, 2022; point-and-click puzzle adventure; siblings Toto and Gal)**
- The core idea was "kids getting lost, partly in their imaginations, and partly in real life." The team began with art, characters and environments and built the hero's journey around them. Co-founder Yuval Markovich: "The story became a matter of, 'OK, a goblin village sounds fun — how do we get there?'" — [Apple Developer, Behind the Design](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
- Fixed story beats were planned early: a dream opening, a return to the house, an undersea scene, a maker space and the goblin village — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
- Transitions between the real and the imagined keep "one foot in fantasy." Example: the siblings pass a park where an old lady feeds pigeons, walk left, and find a goblin in a swamp — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
- The real-world sections carry the sibling-rivalry story. Early versions were "a little too cute," so the team added more picking on each other and a mid-game conflict — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
- The adventure grows out of Gal's play at home: she gets bored and tries to get Toto to play. Each episode's imagined setting (for example, a tomb) frames its puzzles — [TheSixthAxis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/08/10/lost-in-play-review/)
- Example puzzles (from secondary descriptions):
  - Strange frogs hand out swords, and one is trying to pull a sword from a stone.
  - Four small black birds with glowing blue eyes hold game pieces in their beaks in front of a large bird. Toto must climb out on a limb to reach them.
  - Other story beats include riding an enormous bird across the sea and ordering several pizzas.
  - [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
- More example puzzles: a card game against goblins where you bet to win the highest card and reach 30 points, and getting a flag across a pond using rubber ducks — [TheSixthAxis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/08/10/lost-in-play-review/)
- Reviewers also list beating someone at checkers, arranging scenes in order, and deciphering cave paintings. This comes from search-result snippets across several reviews, and attribution to a single review was not verified — [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/61435/lost-in-play-switch-review), [LadiesGamers](https://ladiesgamers.com/lost-in-play-review/)
- The team wanted mini-games and puzzles to feel like episodes of a Saturday-morning cartoon — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)

**Inside (Playdead, 2016)**
- Thomas Grip of Frictional Games calls Inside a "playable story": the player is "an active participant in the transition of the narrative." His examples:
  - **Submarine:** you first hide from a man in a submarine, then sneak past him and steal it. Grip says this "is a lot more powerful than simply having" the player start the next level already in control of a submarine.
  - **Pig:** removing a parasite larva makes a pig friendly and usable in a puzzle, so the lore about parasites is "deeply rooted in the gameplay."
  - **Zombie imitation:** the player must copy the mind-controlled crowd's movements to survive, which lets them "step in the shoes of these zombie people."
  - [Frictional Games blog](https://frictionalgames.blogspot.com/2016/07/thoughts-on-inside-and-playable-stories.html)
- Grip's criticisms:
  - Pressure switches and certain boxes feel like "abstract puzzle devices."
  - Some sections repeat earlier puzzles without adding story, and they "stick out like sore thumbs."
  - Because the game centres on the current puzzle, the world never feels like it "has an agenda of its own."
  - Players rarely learn the layout of locations, so the world "never forms a cohesive whole."
  - [Frictional Games blog](https://frictionalgames.blogspot.com/2016/07/thoughts-on-inside-and-playable-stories.html)
- The body-control mechanic is introduced through a worm and helmet controlling a pig, then a human body. Later, helmets reach the point where the boy controls bodies that are controlling other bodies — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/features/opinion-pieces/puzzle-solutions-and-revolutions-the-overlooked-themes-of-inside), [Epilogue Gaming](https://epiloguegaming.com/how-inside-manifests-the-literary-uncanny-in-video-games/) (search snippets)
- Late in the game the player controls the Huddle, a mass of conjoined bodies that cannot die while under player control but still needs strategy. It ran on a custom 26-body physics simulation plus preset limb animations — [Wikipedia: Inside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game))
- Artist Jeremy Petreman: the overarching story was outlined in advance. Each area kept its core narrative idea, but how the environment conveyed that idea changed often — [Game Developer (summarising Rock Paper Shotgun)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-unstructured-creative-process-that-brought-i-inside-i-to-life)

**The Gardens Between (The Voxel Agents, 2018)**
- Each of the 21 levels is an island representing a shared experience of the two children, scattered with objects along a winding path — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gardens_Between)
- The developers describe two design methods, and most puzzles mix both:
  - **Logical:** draw a timeline on paper for every moving element, using icons for mechanics such as light and dark flowers, chimes, light-phobic bridges and blockers.
  - **Thematic:** start from the setting and story, list its objects (lights switch on and off, see-saws move, radios play music), then decide how time affects each one.
  - [Game Developer Q&A (Simon Joslin, Matt Clark)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-beautifully-surreal-time-puzzles-of-i-the-gardens-between-i-)
- Example, the museum dinosaur garden:
  - The original plan displaced one bone to show a character overstepping boundaries. The final version collapses the whole skeleton.
  - Bones can land on walkable ground, fall into the path, or hit objects to trigger them.
  - Because the physics was hard to control, some simulations were replaced with animations so the landing spots stay consistent.
  - [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-beautifully-surreal-time-puzzles-of-i-the-gardens-between-i-)
- The very first time puzzle was holding time still to burn a rope — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-beautifully-surreal-time-puzzles-of-i-the-gardens-between-i-)
- The playground garden draws on childhood memories of sneaking out at night: playful but a little spooky — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-beautifully-surreal-time-puzzles-of-i-the-gardens-between-i-)

**Planet of Lana I (2023) and II (2026) (Wishfully)**
- In Planet of Lana II, each biome has unique creatures and machines that Mui can hypnotise. Each creature is adapted to its native terrain, and players must work out how to use it in a new area — [GamingBolt interview (Adam Stjärnljus)](https://gamingbolt.com/planet-of-lana-2-children-of-the-leaf-interview-art-direction-varied-environments-hybrid-robots-and-more)
- Creature abilities come from their nature. The tiny ink fish is fast, fits through narrow tunnels, can leap out of water, and makes an ink cloud that hides both the fish and Lana — [Xbox Wire (Dan Faxe, Christian Enfors)](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- Example puzzle: Mui still fears water, but Lana can fetch an underwater bubble plant. Mui enters the bubble and the pair dive together. In one puzzle they must open an underwater "hypno door" without being seen by shark monsters. Extra tunnels were added around the second shark, placed at a tunnel intersection, so players would naturally know where to put an ink cloud — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- The sequel's biomes (snowy mountains, a tropical underwater world, an ancient forest with a village, urban areas) were chosen partly to show how alien technology affects the planet — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/planet-of-lana-2-children-of-the-leaf-interview-art-direction-varied-environments-hybrid-robots-and-more)
- In the first game, ruins grant Lana and Mui new abilities and reveal some lore — [TheSixthAxis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2023/05/22/planet-of-lana-review/)

**Creaks (Amanita Design, 2020)**
- The monsters are built on pareidolia (seeing faces in objects): in light, they turn into the household furniture they resemble. Puzzles use buttons, switches, lights and mechanical devices, and need an understanding of how each monster moves — [Wikipedia: Creaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creaks)
- Lead designer Radim Jurda wanted the place to feel fantastical yet believable, "as if someone had lived there." The furniture was inspired by architect Josef Gočár's cubist designs — search snippets of [Calvert Journal](https://www.calvertjournal.com/articles/show/12150/creaks-video-game-radim-jurda-amanita-design-czech-visual-design) / [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2020/07/interview_amanita_design_on_its_eight_year_journey_making_creaks)
- Jurda: "The key theme of Creaks is ambiguity." The world follows surreal rules but stays logical and cohesive through recurring motifs. Each district of the house combines gameplay rooms and story rooms in a compact layout — [GamingBolt interview](https://gamingbolt.com/creaks-interview-inspiration-development-challenges-future-updates-and-more)
- Story: a boy crawls through a hole behind his bedroom wallpaper into a mansion in a vast cavern that a giant monster made of household objects is destroying. Bird-like residents help him. At the climax, a crystal placed in a lantern makes the light that defeats the monster, so the core light mechanic resolves the plot — [Wikipedia: Creaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creaks)

**Machinarium (Amanita, 2009)**
- Dvorský sometimes reuses classic puzzles such as "five in a row," but only where they fit, and usually modified. In one well-known puzzle a piece is missing and must be found before you can finish — [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-amanita-design-s-dvorsky-on-i-machinarium-i-s-eerie-adventure)
- Only objects within the robot's physical reach can be clicked — [Wikipedia: Machinarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinarium)

**Botanicula (2012) and Samorost 3 (2016) (Amanita)**
- Botanicula has five playable characters with distinct, body-based abilities:
  - Mr. Lantern absorbs seeds and glows.
  - Mrs. Mushroom can make copies of herself and shrink.
  - Mr. Poppyhead is the sturdiest.
  - Mr. Twig can extend many arms and spurt flowers.
  - Mr. Feather is very small but can fly.
  - [Wikipedia: Botanicula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanicula)
- In Samorost 3, the gnome uses a magical flute to solve some puzzles and to help creatures he meets — [Wikipedia: Samorost 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samorost_3)

**Max: The Curse of Brotherhood (Press Play, 2013)**
- Max's spell, found online, gets his younger brother Felix kidnapped by a giant claw, and Max follows him through a portal. The magic marker is the story's central object and puzzle tool — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max:_The_Curse_of_Brotherhood)
- Over the game the marker gains the ability to move earth, grow branches and vines, raise plumes of water and shoot fireballs — search snippet of a review ([GodIsAGeek](https://godisageek.com/2014/01/max-curse-brotherhood-review/) / [Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/reviews/max-curse-brotherhood/); attribution not verified)
- Drawing is limited to pre-set, colour-coded nodes, so Max "can only use his marker to do one of five things." Edge criticised the limits on where you can draw — review search snippets; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max:_The_Curse_of_Brotherhood) (Edge)
- The game has 7 chapters and 20 levels linked into one continuous journey — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max:_The_Curse_of_Brotherhood)

**Bramble: The Mountain King (Dimfrost Studio, 2023)**
- Released 27 April 2023. The boy Olle follows his sister Lillemor, who is captured by a troll and taken to the Mountain King.
- A stone of light is both a puzzle tool and a plot device: it pushes back the Bramble, overcomes the hag Pesta's nightmare, weakens the Bramble binding the King, and revives Olle. Later, Lillemor uses its light to cut her way out of the King's stomach.
- [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble:_The_Mountain_King)
- Set piece: Olle flees the water spirit Näcken along a riverside, and Näcken dies when he follows Olle over a waterfall's edge. This shows a waterfall used as the resolution of a chase rather than as a puzzle — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble:_The_Mountain_King)
- The folklore was chosen because these stories were originally told to keep children away from forests and rivers — [Into Indie Games interview (Fredrik Selldén)](https://intoindiegames.com/features/indie-dev-interview-bramble-the-mountain-king/)
- With no fighting mechanics, every enemy encounter needs some puzzle-based solution (review snippet). PC Gamer said "archaic puzzle design" ruined the experience, and that the puzzles rarely rise above Limbo's early-level puzzles — [Wikipedia (PC Gamer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble:_The_Mountain_King), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/bramble-the-mountain-king-review)

### Inferences
- The recurring successful pattern is to put the puzzle's verb in the plot. The character's next story action (steal the sub, open the hypno door, reassemble the skeleton) *is* the puzzle. For the treasure map game, every puzzle should be an obstacle the siblings would really face. Examples: the front door is locked, so the sister reaches the key on a high hook while the brother crawls through the cat flap to unbolt it. The city bus will leave, so one sibling holds it while the other fetches the ticket.
- Lost in Play's art-first method ("a goblin village sounds fun, how do we get there?") suits a team with strong illustration. List the places you want to draw (house, city street, waterfall, old oak), then ask what real object in each place blocks two children, and which sibling's body solves it.
- Gardens Between's two methods (logical timeline plus thematic object list) carry over directly. For each scene, list its everyday objects (house: stairs, shelves, cat flap, laundry chute, curtains; city: bus stop, fountain, market awning, drainpipe; forest: stepping stones, fallen log, waterfall spray, hollow oak roots). Then decide how "tall" and "small" interact with each.
- Avoid what Grip calls "abstract puzzle devices." In a family storybook setting, a pressure plate should become something diegetic, such as a seesaw, a loose floorboard or a garden tap.
- Grip also faults Inside for never letting players learn the layout of places. A treasure map is a built-in remedy: it can make the world feel spatially coherent.

### Gaps
- No primary Playdead source on Inside's puzzle-by-puzzle design was retrievable. The Rock Paper Shotgun article "How Inside's levels were designed" (May 2017) could not be fetched.
- No precise step-by-step description of a Lost in Play puzzle chain (which item goes where) was found in sources. The examples above are as detailed as the sources got.
- No source was found on how Bramble's sibling relationship shapes individual puzzles.
- Machinarium's stretch/shrink body mechanic (widely known) was not confirmed by any fetched source.

## Q2. How do these games guide players without text?

### Takeaway
All of the primary group are wordless or near-wordless. Each replaces text with a few tools:
- **Tight control vocabularies.** Limbo uses only jump and grab, and Inside has "no superfluous actions."
- **Placement and staging.** Limbo puts the boy next to the object he must use, moves distractions to another screen, and uses sound cues. Planet of Lana II adds tunnels at the right intersection.
- **Animation of the character's intent.** Inside's boy sneaks when he should sneak.
- **Picture-based thought bubbles and gibberish voices.** Machinarium, Samorost 3, Lost in Play.
- **Companions who point or gesture.** Six in Little Nightmares II.

Developers verify all of this by watching first-time players, not by theory.

### Cited Findings
- **Limbo control rule:** Arnt Jensen's goals included a minimalist art style, only jump and grab beyond basic movement, and no tutorial text — [Wikipedia: Limbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game))
- **Limbo boat puzzle:** players did not know to grab the boat. The fix was to place the player near the pullable object and hold them there until they learned the action — [Game Developer, GDC Europe (Jeppe Carlsen)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-europe-i-limbo-i-s-carlsen-on-making-players-your-worst-enemy-and-your-best-friend)
- **Limbo spider and bear trap:** a bear trap on the same screen as a spider drew attention away from the spider, so it was moved to another screen. The sound of the trap falling told players what had happened. When playtesters missed solutions, the team added earlier scenarios that taught the mechanic — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-europe-i-limbo-i-s-carlsen-on-making-players-your-worst-enemy-and-your-best-friend), [Wikipedia: Limbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game))
- **Carlsen on solution clarity:**
  - The correct solution should be fairly easy to execute. If players can't make it work after a few tries, they abandon it, and returning to an abandoned idea takes a long time.
  - Wrong approaches should clearly look wrong. Ones that come close hold players' attention too long.
  - Misdirection is used deliberately: show an element the player will certainly use, then a false solution that looks right.
  - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-europe-i-limbo-i-s-carlsen-on-making-players-your-worst-enemy-and-your-best-friend)
- **Inside:**
  - "Inside doesn't have any superfluous actions," so players can quickly ask "Can I interact with it?", which cuts trial and error.
  - Adaptive animation signals intent: when the boy must sneak, his animation shows it, so "the player can shift their mindset to match that of the boy's."
  - The boy waves his torch automatically when dogs approach, and the loss of control "feels totally OK."
  - [Frictional Games blog](https://frictionalgames.blogspot.com/2016/07/thoughts-on-inside-and-playable-stories.html)
- **Inside's sound and small reactions:**
  - Some Inside puzzles are built around visual and audio cues. Composer Martin Stig Andersen worked with the gameplay and design teams on structure and pacing — [Wikipedia: Inside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game))
  - Small reactions from the boy, such as looking at the lines of mind-controlled people, make him feel alive. The game uses almost no score, relying on sound design (creaking wood, damp concrete) — [CogConnected](https://cogconnected.com/feature/inside-turns-bad-game-design-head/)
- **Playdead "no talking" rule:** Jeppe Carlsen reportedly said the nonverbal constraint came from director Arnt Jensen and was "a company rule of sorts." This is a search snippet that appears to come from a [premortem.games interview](https://premortem.games/2024/09/18/cocoon-creator-jeppe-carlsen-i-never-compromise-on-playability/), not fetched.
- **Machinarium:**
  - No spoken or written dialogue apart from a few tutorial prompts on the first screen. Animated thought bubbles communicate objectives, and backstory flashbacks appear as thought bubbles only when the player idles in certain areas — [Wikipedia: Machinarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinarium)
  - Clues are meant to be somewhat obvious. A "No robots" sign signals where the robot should go — [Game Developer (Dvorský)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-amanita-design-s-dvorsky-on-i-machinarium-i-s-eerie-adventure)
- **Samorost 3:** no comprehensible dialogue, only animated thought bubbles and gibberish — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samorost_3). Dvorský says the studio does not like text or lengthy dialogue and considers this part of its design approach — [MCV interview](https://mcvuk.com/interview-amanita-design/)
- **Lost in Play:**
  - No dialogue; characters speak gibberish. The team used its TV and film animation training in scriptwriting, cinematography and acting to convey meaning visually. Cartoon techniques carry tone, for example the camera shakes when something is scary — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
  - Players rely on visuals and the environment to read what is going on, and humour is mostly slapstick — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/08/10/lost-in-play-review/)
- **Creaks:** with no words in the game, the team "made the storyboard very detailed" and tested it on friends to see if the story landed from pictures alone (Radim Jurda) — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/creaks-interview-inspiration-development-challenges-future-updates-and-more)
- **The Gardens Between:** "We tell the whole story without any text or dialog" — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-beautifully-surreal-time-puzzles-of-i-the-gardens-between-i-)
- **Planet of Lana:**
  - The story is wordless, and human characters speak an invented language, so communication relies on sound and visuals — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2023/05/22/planet-of-lana-review/)
  - Lana II's puzzle reviews ask whether the goal is clear, whether failure gives enough feedback to avoid getting stuck, and whether elements are introduced in the right order. Level art and lighting are added only after a puzzle tests well — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- **Little Nightmares II:** Six points out objects of interest and acts as a pointer for the next step, giving "subtle cues" if you're lost — [Press Start review](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/playstation4-reviews/2021/02/10/little-nightmares-ii-review-misery-loves-company/). Another review says she will "gesture towards hints and solutions" (review search snippet).

### Inferences
- Keep each sibling's verb set tiny and readable, so players can always ask "can I interact with this?":
  - Sister: reach, lift, boost.
  - Brother: crawl, squeeze, ride on shoulders.
- Use Limbo's placement fix for first-time teaching. Introduce "sister reaches the high hook" in a room where the hook, and nothing else, is in reach, and hold the camera there.
- With split information, the "pointing companion" idea (Six) becomes a player-to-player tool. A diegetic point/look/wave gesture, with the character turning its head toward the hotspot, lets one player guide the other without text chat. Inside's "boy looks at things" animation is the same idea done by the game.
- Gibberish voices plus picture thought bubbles (Machinarium, Samorost 3, Lost in Play) suit a family audience and avoid localisation. A sibling who "thinks" a picture of the key shows the partner what they've seen, without words.
- Follow the Creaks practice and storyboard every scene, then test it on people who haven't seen it before writing code.

### Gaps
- No source detailed lighting and colour as guidance in these games (for example, Limbo/Inside's use of light to frame the interactable). The idea is widely discussed but no citable source was retrieved.
- No game-design video essays were retrieved (searches returned only text articles). Game Maker's Toolkit and similar channels were not verified.

## Q3. How do they pace puzzle density against walking, chases, spectacle and calm?

### Takeaway
These games alternate puzzle rooms with relief: story cutscenes, walking vistas, sit-down moments, set-piece chases and collectible "reward" rooms. They keep one unbroken journey (no loading screens, linked levels). Difficulty ramps by layering mechanics already learned, and new elements are tested in sequence because too many at once spikes difficulty. Common complaints are long empty walks (Planet of Lana), repetitive chases and trial-and-error deaths (Little Nightmares II, Inside), and puzzles that are too easy (Bramble, late Gardens Between).

### Cited Findings
- **Creaks:** Jurda: "adding those story cutscenes or mechanical paintings brings the elements of relief and reward between each puzzle section," because constant riddles are hard on players. Each district combines gameplay rooms and story rooms — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/creaks-interview-inspiration-development-challenges-future-updates-and-more)
- **Inside:** "Inside doesn't even feature any loading screens." It is "one continuous narrative" with player control almost throughout. Background events play out while you keep control, and being spotted can be dangerous, which gives "a certain 'realness'" — [Frictional Games blog](https://frictionalgames.blogspot.com/2016/07/thoughts-on-inside-and-playable-stories.html). Composer Martin Stig Andersen helped plan structure and pacing so that scenes build tension — [Wikipedia: Inside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game)). The game is about three hours long — [CogConnected](https://cogconnected.com/feature/inside-turns-bad-game-design-head/)
- **Max:** 20 levels linked so the game plays as one continuous journey. Destructoid praised the mix of thoughtful puzzles with "thrilling action sequences" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max:_The_Curse_of_Brotherhood)
- **Planet of Lana I:**
  - Long sections are mostly moving right and running, which emphasises journey over puzzles. Lana and Mui sometimes sit down so the player can take in the view, and the camera often pulls back to show scale. Getting caught in stealth is instant death. Criticisms are long stretches of just pushing right and repeated greenery and rock areas. About four hours — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2023/05/22/planet-of-lana-review/)
  - Other estimates are under five hours to six to eight hours (review snippets). Stjärnljus describes the series' balance as "tension, stealth, cozy puzzle-solving, and serene cinematic moments" (search snippet of a 2026 interview, not fetched).
- **Planet of Lana II:**
  - Puzzles must balance challenge and flow, and the team watches for anything that breaks rhythm. Introducing too many new elements at once could make a puzzle much harder than intended, so playtests run in the context of the full game.
  - Testers watch for confusion and frustration, but "a stalled player can be fine if they're having fun and generating new ideas."
  - [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
  - Lana can now dive, adding verticality and tension from the need to breathe — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- **Little Nightmares II:**
  - The core loop is entering a room, working out what can be interacted with, and using that to reach the next room. Major enemy encounters are usually "an intense chase sequence."
  - Frequent instant deaths on entering rooms make chases go from tense to repetitive, and trial and error "bogs down" the game. About 5 to 6 hours versus about 4 for the original.
  - [Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/playstation4-reviews/2021/02/10/little-nightmares-ii-review-misery-loves-company/)
- **The Gardens Between:** puzzles start easy and grow harder, building on earlier concepts. The museum garden was built last, after the team had learned what made gardens work — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-beautifully-surreal-time-puzzles-of-i-the-gardens-between-i-). Some late puzzles were judged too easy, and the game is "short but sweet" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gardens_Between)
- **Limbo:** Playdead called the core loop "trial and death." Gruesome but entertaining deaths discouraged wrong solutions without real penalty, and checkpoints allow unlimited restarts — [Wikipedia: Limbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game)). Carlsen calls it a "learning-by-dying game" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-europe-i-limbo-i-s-carlsen-on-making-players-your-worst-enemy-and-your-best-friend)
- **Limbo and Inside critiques:** "Trial-and-error wears down on the player's stamina." Repeat-mechanic filler sections stick out — [Frictional Games blog](https://frictionalgames.blogspot.com/2016/07/thoughts-on-inside-and-playable-stories.html)
- **Bramble:** travel is mostly linear, with occasional puzzles or platform jumps and occasional action sequences with boss fights — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble:_The_Mountain_King). Gamereactor felt there "aren't any particularly complex" puzzles (review snippet, [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/bramble-the-mountain-king-1262983/))
- **Botanicula:** compared with Machinarium, it has fewer or less tough puzzles and leans on exploration (Czech Radio snippet, [Radio Prague International](https://english.radio.cz/node/8262771); page blocked from fetch)

### Inferences
- A rhythm that suits a family co-op game: puzzle room, relief beat (sit-down vista or cutscene), puzzle room, light chase or "run together" set piece, then a calm reward or collectible room. The sit-down moment in Planet of Lana works especially well in co-op, because both players' characters sit together.
- For a family audience, drop Limbo/Inside-style instant-death trial and error, which reviewers fault even in adult games. Use soft failure (slip back, splash into the stream, the cat chases you back to the start of the room).
- Test new mechanics in sequence inside the full flow, as Lana II does. In a co-op split-ability game, a new "reach" idea and a new "crawl" idea arriving together doubles the load on both players.
- Thinking time matters more online, where partners also talk to each other. Lana II's view that "a stalled player can be fine if they're having fun" suggests idle animations and toys in puzzle rooms, so being stuck feels like play.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data (puzzles per minute, ratio of walking to puzzle time) was found for any game.
- No source on Lost in Play's chapter pacing beyond the fixed story beats.

## Q4. How do they use companions, animals or NPCs as puzzle elements?

### Takeaway
Companion puzzles fall into a few types:
- **Autonomous ally with light commands.** Mui (follow, stay, go there) and Six (call, hold hands).
- **Body-swap control of creatures or bodies.** Lana II's hypnotised animals and Inside's mind-control helmets.
- **Asymmetric party abilities.** Botanicula's five characters, Gardens Between's lantern carrier and chime ringer.
- **NPCs as rule-bound hazards or helpers.** Creaks' furniture monsters, the residents of Creaks, the goblins of Lost in Play.

Companions also serve the story: their weakness creates escort puzzles (Mui's fear of water), and the player's lack of control over them carries emotional weight (Six).

### Cited Findings
- **Planet of Lana I (Mui):**
  - Creative director Adam Stjärnljus said Mui acts on its own until given a command, so Lana doesn't switch control between the two characters (search snippet of an interview; Escapist page not fetched). Mui can tame some hostile creatures and hack machines (Wikipedia snippet, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_of_Lana)).
  - Players give simple commands such as "stay" or "follow," or point to a nearby place and send Mui there. Mui can distract spider-like droids or change water levels so Lana avoids detection (review snippets, for example [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/planet-of-lana-review), [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/planet-of-lana-review-spirited-away); attribution not verified).
  - Mui leaps to higher ledges to nudge ropes free, sits among dark tendrils to trigger a creature into action, and triggers switches. Mui dislikes water, so you must work around that, and you can lose Mui through carelessness — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2023/05/22/planet-of-lana-review/)
  - Sometimes "you'll have to put work in to provide Mui a safe path through an area" (review snippet).
- **Planet of Lana II:**
  - In the first game, animals were controlled indirectly by constraining them to Mui's position. In the sequel, the player controls animals directly and independently of Mui — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
  - Players can point and direct Mui anywhere on screen, which enables bigger puzzles. Lana's and Mui's relative positions matter. Mui's jamming ability temporarily disables hostile robots, and Lana can hack and control some of them — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/planet-of-lana-2-children-of-the-leaf-interview-art-direction-varied-environments-hybrid-robots-and-more)
  - Mui hacks machines and briefly controls creatures, and Lana can wall jump, dash and slide — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_of_Lana_II)
- **Little Nightmares II (Six):**
  - Six is computer-controlled. The player can call out to her and hold her hand to stay together. The pair cooperate on environmental puzzles and enemy encounters, for example operating a ranged weapon together — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nightmares_II)
  - She boosts Mono up to high places and sometimes gives subtle hints. Hand-holding is optional and solves no puzzle. Mono's voice is used only to call Six, which "doesn't always work" (review snippets: [TrueAchievements reviews](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Little-Nightmares-II/reviews), [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/little-nightmares-2-review), others; attribution not verified)
  - Her AI sometimes froze and ignored commands ("Buggy Buddy AI") — [Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/playstation4-reviews/2021/02/10/little-nightmares-ii-review-misery-loves-company/)
  - Lead producer Lucas Roussel: making Six an AI companion "allowed for a more complex level design and longer puzzles that would rely on players ability to understand Six," and "players are never fully in charge of Six' destiny" — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2021/03/12/little-nightmares-2-interview/)
  - Co-op was considered early (one player as Mono, one as Six) but dropped because it clashed with the tightly produced design and the story. The team also nearly cut Six altogether (senior narrative designer Dave Mervik) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nightmares_II); [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/tarsier-studios-little-nightmares-2-no-co-op-support) and [Escapist](https://escapistmagazine.com/?p=17435) (snippets)
- **The Gardens Between (Arina and Frendt):**
  - The player never moves the children directly. They scrub time forward and back, and the pair walk the path as time advances.
  - Arina carries a lantern that absorbs light, opens the end portal and clears solid purple fog. She can hand the lantern to cube-shaped creatures and pick it up later.
  - Frendt rings chimes that open or close flowers holding light or dark spheres. He also activates devices (a radio, a garden hose) that let the player reverse time for that one object.
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gardens_Between)
- **Inside (NPC bodies as tools):**
  - Mind-control helmets let the boy control bodies to solve puzzles — [Wikipedia: Inside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game))
  - A de-parasited pig becomes a puzzle tool. The player must imitate the zombie crowd to survive — [Frictional Games blog](https://frictionalgames.blogspot.com/2016/07/thoughts-on-inside-and-playable-stories.html)
- **Limbo:** glowing worms attach to the boy's head and force him to move in one direction until killed. Later, a bear trap that killed you earlier is reused to free a ledge — [Wikipedia: Limbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game))
- **Botanicula:** five characters with distinct abilities (glow, duplicate or shrink, sturdiness, extending arms, flying). Players can interact with many other creatures — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanicula)
- **Creaks:**
  - Monsters become furniture in light, and solving puzzles requires understanding how each one moves and behaves — [Wikipedia: Creaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creaks)
  - Bird-like residents help with the plot: an elder seeking a codex, a fighter who sets traps, a scientist trying to tame the creature, and a mechanic — [Wikipedia: Creaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creaks)
- **Lost in Play:** Toto and Gal work together to solve problems but get annoyed with each other when the situation calls for it — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/08/10/lost-in-play-review/). NPCs are puzzle owners: frogs with swords, ravens holding game pieces, gambling goblins — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
- **Bramble:** Lemus, a stone giant, becomes an ally. The team says it added "small charming mechanics here and there you can use to interact with creatures" (Fredrik Selldén) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble:_The_Mountain_King), [Into Indie Games](https://intoindiegames.com/features/indie-dev-interview-bramble-the-mountain-king/)
- **Max:** the brother Felix is the goal (rescue target), not a puzzle partner — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max:_The_Curse_of_Brotherhood)

### Inferences
- Little Nightmares II is the closest single-player template for the design. Tarsier found that a companion who boosts, holds hands and cooperates on two-person mechanisms allowed "longer puzzles." They dropped co-op because their tight, scripted design clashed with it. A co-op game must therefore make the scripted moments robust to two free agents: avoid Inside-style precisely timed set pieces that depend on a single actor.
- The useful verbs from these companions, mapped to the siblings:
  - From Six: boost up, catch a jump, hold hands to move together, cooperate on a two-person mechanism. These become sister-boosts-brother and brother-on-shoulders.
  - From Mui: go where only the small one fits, and be afraid of something the other must solve. These map to the brother's crawl-through-gaps ability.
- The Gardens Between shows asymmetric roles on a shared puzzle, one carrying light and one ringing chimes. That matches a split-ability co-op design, with each child's action changing the state the other needs.
- Weakness as a puzzle generator (Mui's fear of water, the bubble plant) has a direct sibling version. The brother is afraid of the dark or the barking dog, so the sister must make a safe path. Equally, the sister is too big for the tunnel, so the brother must open the gate from the inside.
- Lana II's change from indirect to direct creature control, which allowed "bigger puzzles," suggests that animal helpers (a city pigeon, a forest squirrel) could be controlled briefly by one player while the other moves on. That creates natural split-attention moments.

### Gaps
- No primary Tarsier source on specific Six puzzles (for example, a step-by-step two-person mechanism) was retrieved.
- No source confirmed how Planet of Lana I's command input works (button mapping, contextual cursor).

## Q5. How do they handle collectibles, secrets, optional puzzles and replay?

### Takeaway
Collectibles in these games are mostly optional, story-flavoured secrets rather than currency:
- Inside's hidden orbs unlock an alternate ending.
- Little Nightmares II has hats and "glitching remains" that unlock a scene.
- Creaks has hidden paintings that hold minigames.
- Limbo has hidden eggs.
- Amanita games have hidden animations and Easter eggs, framed as playing with a toy.

Replay value comes from exploration and secrets, because single-solution puzzles have little replay value in themselves.

### Cited Findings
- **Inside:** hidden rooms contain glowing orbs, and deactivating all of them unlocks the alternate ending — [Wikipedia: Inside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game))
- **Limbo:** optional achievements for finding hidden insect eggs and finishing with five or fewer deaths — [Wikipedia: Limbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game)). Carlsen notes the puzzles have no replay value, so their enjoyment comes from solving them — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-europe-i-limbo-i-s-carlsen-on-making-players-your-worst-enemy-and-your-best-friend)
- **Little Nightmares II:** collectible hats and glitching remains, and collecting all the remains unlocks an extra scene — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nightmares_II)
- **Creaks:**
  - Hidden 18th- and 19th-century-style paintings act as collectibles, and many contain minigames — [Wikipedia: Creaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creaks)
  - They reuse the book-searching motif of the main story and act as "relief and reward" between puzzle sections — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/creaks-interview-inspiration-development-challenges-future-updates-and-more)
- **Samorost 3 (Dvorský):** "something like a huge interactive toy." Hidden animations and Easter eggs aren't needed to finish. Since finishing isn't the only goal, players return to places and explore more carefully, which he thinks makes the genre "more profound and relaxing" — [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/interview-amanita-design/)
- **Machinarium:** Dvorský wants a game that "will feel like a toy that you can play with" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-amanita-design-s-dvorsky-on-i-machinarium-i-s-eerie-adventure)
- **Botanicula:** players can find hidden Easter eggs and interact with many creatures — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanicula)
- **Lost in Play:** some puzzles and mini-games have a Skip button (review snippet, attribution not verified; see [Switchaboo](https://switchaboo.com/lost-in-play-switch-review))

### Inferences
- The treasure map frame is a natural collectible container, for example torn map corners, the siblings' stickers, or drawings in their journal. Unlike Inside's orbs, these can reward cooperation: a secret needs both siblings' abilities, so it is a small optional co-op puzzle.
- Amanita's "interactive toy" approach (clickable objects with funny animations and no reward) gives the player who is waiting during a split-information moment something to do. It also extends the play value of illustrated scenes.
- A per-scene skip, as in Lost in Play, suits family play where a younger player may stall.

### Gaps
- No source on whether Lost in Play has collectibles or achievements tied to secrets.
- No source on Planet of Lana collectibles (the in-game murals and lore are noted as ability ruins only).

## Q6. How do they handle being stuck (hint systems)?

### Takeaway
The point-and-click side (Amanita, Lost in Play) builds hints into the game with deliberate friction:
- Machinarium has a free vague picture hint, then a full sketch walkthrough of the current screen only, unlocked by a small minigame.
- Samorost 3 uses the same minigame-gated sketches.
- Lost in Play has picture hints that are "challenges in themselves," plus some skippable minigames.

The platformer side (Limbo, Inside, Creaks) gives no hints and relies on clean design, placement and fast retries. Players and reviewers notice the absence (Creaks' Discord community, Well-Played's criticism).

### Cited Findings
- **Machinarium:**
  - Each level offers one hint, which grows vaguer as the game goes on. A minigame unlocks a walkthrough that uses sketches to show each puzzle's solution — [Wikipedia: Machinarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinarium)
  - The walkthrough minigame is a crude scrolling shooter with Game Boy-style graphics in which you guide a key through spider-infested tunnels. The free hint is a thought bubble showing an important object or end goal. The book does not tell you how to get items from other places — [Wurb, "Machinarium: Gameplay and Hints"](https://wurb.com/stack/archives/1924) (via search snippets)
  - Dvorský: the walkthrough is a hand-drawn comic, more appealing than online walkthroughs. You must win a small minigame to open it, and it opens only the page for your current location, "so it is less convenient" to use as a full solution — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-amanita-design-s-dvorsky-on-i-machinarium-i-s-eerie-adventure)
  - Glenn White: the minigame "may not be hugely challenging," but the extra step makes players reconsider. Keeping it in the game stops players from reading too much of an online guide. Designers should give "just enough information to get them to where they need without removing the challenge" — [Game Developer blog](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-solve-a-problem-like-machinarium)
- **Botanicula:** dropped the help book because the team felt it wasn't needed for a gentler, exploration-led game (Czech Radio snippet, [Radio Prague International](https://english.radio.cz/node/8262771))
- **Samorost 3:**
  - The walkthrough is accessed through a minigame and presented as sketches — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samorost_3)
  - The Washington Post praised the hint system for sparing players frustration — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samorost_3)
- **Lost in Play:**
  - The hint system was designed to avoid the old text-adventure problem of getting "stuck for months." Hints don't just give the answer; they "present challenges in themselves," which gives "a feeling of accomplishment," and the team wanted to keep players from searching online — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
  - Reviewers describe hints as a single illustration of what to do next, opened on Switch by holding Y (review snippets, attribution not verified)
  - TheSixthAxis calls them "generous": they point you toward what to look for without giving the full answer — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/08/10/lost-in-play-review/)
  - Nintendo World Report felt some puzzles made the hint system necessary or had solutions that didn't make sense (snippet, [NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/61435/lost-in-play-switch-review))
  - A Japanese developer interview with Alon Simon is headlined, roughly, "the puzzles may be a little too difficult for children" — [Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2022/09/09/122164.html) (headline only, body not read)
- **Limbo:** no hint system and no menus. "we didn't want to have any in-game menus or anything," so players can get stuck. Carlsen's playtest notes reduce to whether testers got stuck in a "frustration loop: yes or no" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-europe-i-limbo-i-s-carlsen-on-making-players-your-worst-enemy-and-your-best-friend); [Escapist GDC 2011 coverage](https://escapistmagazine.com/gdc-2011-limbos-puzzle-creator-is-your-enemy) (snippet)
- **Creaks:**
  - No hint system. Players help each other on Discord, and numbered scenes were planned in an update to make that easier — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/creaks-interview-inspiration-development-challenges-future-updates-and-more)
  - Well-Played criticised the lack of a hint system — [Wikipedia: Creaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creaks)
- **Planet of Lana II:** design reviews ask whether failure gives enough feedback to avoid getting stuck — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- **Machinarium:** Dvorský: "it must be funny to fail, basically" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-amanita-design-s-dvorsky-on-i-machinarium-i-s-eerie-adventure)

### Inferences
- For a storybook family co-op game, a diegetic hint object fits the frame: the treasure map itself, or a sibling's sketchbook. Following Machinarium, the hint should show only the current scene, in pictures, behind a small bit of friction. The friction should be a tiny co-op action (both players tap the map together), so neither player can spoil it alone.
- Use tiers, as Machinarium does: a free vague picture first, then the full sketch.
- With split information, the hint can go only to the player whose half is relevant. That keeps the "explain it to your partner" part of play.
- Make failure funny, not punishing (Dvorský). That reduces the stuck-feeling that hints exist to fix.
- Numbered scenes (the Creaks patch) are a cheap way for families to look up help.

### Gaps
- The exact mechanics of Lost in Play's hint challenge (what the "challenge" is) were not found in any fetched source.
- No source on whether Little Nightmares II or Planet of Lana have any assist or hint options.

## Q7. What do developers say about their puzzle design process?

### Takeaway
Developers describe four consistent practices:
- **Design on paper or storyboards first:** Gardens Between timelines, Lana II overview sketches, Creaks storyboards.
- **Design adversarially, then fair:** Carlsen's "worst enemy, then best friend."
- **Cut hard:** Limbo cut about 70 percent of its content, and Inside's ideas went through "natural selection."
- **Test constantly with first-time players:** in context, watching for confusion and frustration, with art added only after a puzzle works.

### Cited Findings
- **Jeppe Carlsen (Limbo, GDC Europe 2010 "Limbo: Balancing Fun and Frustration in Puzzle Design"):**
  - "think of the player as your worst enemy." Design the most devious puzzle, then switch roles and give the player the clues needed to solve it fairly.
  - Start difficult and scale back, keeping only the useful element of each over-complex design: "we had to throw out a lot of work all the time."
  - The biggest rewards come from finally solving a puzzle you struggled with.
  - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-europe-i-limbo-i-s-carlsen-on-making-players-your-worst-enemy-and-your-best-friend); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1013665/Limbo-Balancing-Fun-and-Frustration)
- **Limbo cuts:** Carlsen aimed for puzzles that were neither too simple nor so convoluted that players guessed without understanding. One example uses only a switch panel, an electrified floor and a chain. Co-founder Dino Patti said they "trashed 70%" of the content they developed — [Wikipedia: Limbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game))
- **Inside (Jeremy Petreman, artist):** "All the efficiency and organization was dropped in favor of a purely creative, open, and iterative process." Environments passed between artists with little discussion. "The good ideas just sort of rose to the top, and the mediocre ones ended up naturally falling away" — [Game Developer (via RPS)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-unstructured-creative-process-that-brought-i-inside-i-to-life)
- **Planet of Lana II (Dan Faxe, Christian Enfors):** the pipeline is:
  1. Overview sketches are presented to the whole team, walked through in stages.
  2. Reviewers check goal clarity, failure feedback and introduction order.
  3. The puzzle is revised until the team is confident.
  4. Level designers build it in the engine, checking scale and distances.
  5. Early playtests use players who haven't seen the sketches, inside the full game sequence.
  6. Level artists add detail and lighting only after the puzzle tests well.
  - The final shark puzzle kept the sketch's shape, with changed proportions and distances.
  - [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/03/planet-of-lana-ii-puzzle-design/)
- **The Gardens Between (Joslin, Clark):**
  - The first prototype was inspired by the Minority Report scene where a character scrubs through a memory. Early puzzles borrowed from hidden-object games.
  - The team then narrowed to two characters climbing an island, designed with paper timelines and branching notation.
  - [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-beautifully-surreal-time-puzzles-of-i-the-gardens-between-i-)
  - Director Henrik Pettersson said the time mechanic began as a small Little Red Riding Hood demo — [WellPlayed](https://well-played.com.au/made-in-australia-we-talk-the-gardens-between-with-the-voxel-agents) (snippet)
- **Lost in Play (Happy Juice Games):**
  - "It's hard to predict a gaming experience other than watching a player play for the first time." The team built many mini-games and aimed for a difficulty that suits a family audience. Gameplay creation was "a bit chaotic," and the children are based on co-founder Oren Rubin's own children — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/happy-juice-games-production-of-animation-quest-games)
  - Rubin focused on keeping it "comic, funny, and easy": "the best thing we hear is that it's a game parents enjoy playing with their kids." The team was 7 people — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=n4w6zydm)
  - The developers sketched ideas to find quickly whether they worked, and narrowing down the many imagination ideas was a challenge — [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/dive-into-a-world-of-pure-imagination-with-point-and-click-adventure-lost-in) (snippet)
- **Amanita (Jakub Dvorský):**
  - Machinarium's puzzles are more logical than earlier Amanita games: "It's a lot about thinking about the puzzles, not just trying randomly and clicking everything."
  - Players carry only a few items and can't reach certain locations, which limits options per area. He aims for "something in between" old adventure games' endless options and modern ones that are too easy.
  - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-amanita-design-s-dvorsky-on-i-machinarium-i-s-eerie-adventure)
  - For Samorost 3, he wanted it easier for inexperienced players than Machinarium, with music and sound playing a bigger role in gameplay — [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/interview-amanita-design/)
- **Creaks (Radim Jurda):** began as his diploma work at Prague's Academy of Arts, Architecture and Design, with Flash prototypes made with Jan Chlup. It took about eight years — [Pocket Gamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/74149/indie-spotlight-amanita-design-radim-jurda-creaks/), [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2020/07/interview_amanita_design_on_its_eight_year_journey_making_creaks) (snippets)
- **Bramble (Fredrik Selldén):** "We combine platforming with puzzles and big boss fights." Inspirations included Brothers, Inside, Limbo, Unravel and Little Nightmares — [Into Indie Games](https://intoindiegames.com/features/indie-dev-interview-bramble-the-mountain-king/)

### Inferences
- A pipeline that fits a small co-op team: an overview sketch per puzzle (Lana II), split into each player's screen view to check the split information. Then a whitebox test with two first-time players in the full sequence, then art and lighting. Watch both players and log "frustration loop: yes/no" per player (Carlsen).
- Carlsen's "worst enemy, then best friend" step maps onto split information. Design what each player can't see (enemy), then design the gestures and visible clues that let them share it (friend).
- Expect to cut a lot of content, as Limbo did. Puzzles that need explaining, or that only one sibling can solve while the other waits, are the first to cut in a co-op design.

### Gaps
- No Playdead GDC talk specifically on Inside's puzzle design was found. The search found only Limbo talks (2010, 2011); Inside's GDC talks were on rendering and audio (not verified in this session).
- No Happy Juice Games GDC or postmortem talk was found.
- The "if time allows" games (Gris, Toem, Chicory, Lumino City, Hob, Oddmar, Ori, Teslagrad, Fez, Braid) and Little Nightmares I were not researched for time reasons.
