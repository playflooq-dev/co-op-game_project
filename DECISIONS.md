## Core concept

| # | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Genre: co-op puzzle game** | Chosen over action, survival and party games. |
| 2 | **Exactly 2 players** | Made to play with a friend or partner. |
| 3 | **Each player on their own phone, online** | No shared-screen mode. Players connect over the internet. |
| 4 | **Split abilities** | Each player can do different things, so both are needed to solve puzzles. |
| 5 | **Split information** | Each player sees things the other doesn't, so they have to communicate. Kept light, because the audience doesn't usually play games. |
| 6 | **No puzzle can be solved alone** | One player makes or reveals the path, and the other uses it. |

## Originality

| # | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | **Everything comes from real life** | Worlds, art style and mechanics come from crafts, places and materials, never from other games. |
| 12 | **No first-pick AI ideas** | For example stained glass, glowing silhouettes, lanterns or mirror worlds. |
| 13 | **Real references only** | Never invent a reference. |

| # | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | **Web app in the phone browser** | Online from the start. App-store versions may come later. |
| 15 | **30 fps or more on a phone** | |
| 16 | **three.js, 2.5D scenes, gameplay on a flat 2D plane** | |
| 17 | **Thin, separate online layer** | The client plans one shared multiplayer system later, so the game only sends simple messages that are easy to move to that system. |

## How each world is made

1. Gather real references for the craft.
2. Take the co-op mechanic from the craft's making process.
3. Fable 5.2 builds the scene in three.js and reviews its own screenshots for at least three passes.
4. Opus 5.5 gives a second review.
5. The scene is checked on a phone.
6. A simple two-phone connection goes in.
7. The puzzles go in.

Big ideas are described before they are built.
