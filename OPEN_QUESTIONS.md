# Open Questions

Things still to decide. When one is agreed, move it to [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) and delete it here.
What is already approved is not repeated here: see DECISIONS.md, and [PROJECT_STATUS.md](PROJECT_STATUS.md) for where
the game stands.

Chapter 1 (the park, the city, the canal) was approved on 2026-11-25 "for now": it is good enough to build on, and
the points below are what could still change in it.

## 1. Small decisions waiting in the three levels

Each was found in a first-time player review. Nothing here blocks playing.

**Level 1, the park** (the full list, 29 items, is in [game/worlds/park/REVIEW.md](game/worlds/park/REVIEW.md)):
- The cat on the box in section 3: it sits on the one thing that has to be picked up. Take it off the box?
- The birds inside the two gate puzzles: they sit where the second player has to walk. Remove those two flocks?
- The lift works from section 2 on, although it is only taught at the balloon tree in section 5. Keep it off until then?
- The pond has a hand sign but no task. Do players search it for a puzzle?
- Section 3 puts the box behind the gate so that one sibling cannot solve it alone. One step too many for the very
  first box?

**Level 2, the city:**
- It was built as a test level before the progression rules were written, so it has eight stations and five new
  ideas in a row (heavy crate, see-saw, boats, pulley, toss). Is that the right size for a second level, or should it
  be shortened or split later?
- The pulley station can get into a muddle if boxes are dropped to the ground. It can always be undone, but not
  obviously. Simplify it?
- In Level 3 she is stopped from hopping from boat to boat by jumping the instant she lands. Level 2's boats do not
  have that rule, and nobody has tested whether she can cross its canal that way. Test it, and close it if so?

**Level 3, the canal** (the full list, 22 items, is in [game/worlds/canal/REVIEW.md](game/worlds/canal/REVIEW.md)):
- Level 2 already has small boats, a ferry and the same Old Bridge with two levers, so light-only surfaces and the
  ending are a second meeting for players. Accept that, or later end Level 2 before its canal station and somewhere
  other than the bridge?
- In section 5 he can stand on the plate himself, so the box is not strictly needed. Leave it?
- There is no toss in Level 3, although Level 2 taught it. Keep it out?
- The calm moment has no dog and no ball. Bring them back there, or keep it plain?
- Three screens follow the last puzzle (the end card, the map, the chapter card). Too many?

**All three levels:**
- Whoever runs ahead is stopped at the edge of the picture, and nothing shows why. Add a small picture that points
  back to the other sibling?
- The low end of the see-saw can only be walked onto at its tip (jumping onto it works anywhere). Change the shared
  see-saw?
- The end cards show the time. Does a time read as a score ("be faster")? Drop it?
- The shop signs painted into the art have words on them ("Books", "Café", "Bridge Control"). Nobody has to read
  them. Replace them with pictures one day?

## 2. What nobody has tested yet

Everything so far was checked by scripts that press the keys, and by the user and the client playing on a computer.
Still to do:
- **Children of 5 to 10**, with a parent and with each other. Where do they stand still for more than ten seconds?
  Which hint gets them going (sparkle, arrow, ghost)? Where do they laugh? Does anybody only wait?
- **A real tablet and a real phone.** The touch buttons were only checked at tablet and phone sizes on a computer.
  Are four buttons each reachable for two people holding one tablet? Is the picture big enough on a phone?
- **One person playing both siblings.** The Old Bridge needs two keys held at once.
- **Sound by ear**: do the pieces suit each place, and are the volumes right?
- **How long the levels really take.** The scripts need about 2, 2.5 and 2 minutes; the guesses for real players
  are about 5, 10 and 8 minutes.
- **The chapter map**: is it understood without words (tick, arrow, padlock; tap or jump to start; replay)? Do
  players find their way on when they come back the next day?

## 3. The story

- **The house comes first, and has to be reviewed before release.** Since 2026-11-27 the house, "Home Alone"
  ([game/house.html](game/house.html)), is the first stop on the chapter map: new players start there, and the park
  opens after it. So the story begins at home again (a treasure map on the kitchen table, escaping the house). But
  the house was built with the older rules, and nothing in it was changed. Before the game is released it needs a
  review against [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md) and the "nothing needs reading" rule, because it is now the first
  level players see. What that review will have to deal with:
  - **It needs reading.** The opening card, the parents' letter, what the siblings say, the line at the top that
    says what to do, the hints over their heads and the end card are all words.
  - **There is no jump, and the keys mean something else.** In the house, up is "use" or "climb" and down is "go
    down" or "crawl in". In the park, right after it, up is "jump" and down is "grab". The park then teaches the keys
    as if nobody had played before.
  - **Its puzzle is a code**: he reads three pictures in the dark cupboard and tells her, and she sets them on the
    box. That is a harder first puzzle than anything in the park, and it is not an idea that comes back later.
  - **Its cards say "Chapter One" and "Chapter One complete"**, although the chapter goes on with three more levels.
    (Only the last line of the end card was changed, from "go on to Chapter Two" to "go on".)
  - **Who is it for?** A child of 5 cannot read it; a parent would have to read it out.
  - **What to do with it:** rebuild it the new way as a short, wordless opening; keep it but take the words out; or
    move it off the main path again.
- **How the levels are numbered.** The park, the city and the canal are still called Level 1, 2 and 3 everywhere,
  and the house is called "the opening". Should the house be Level 1, and the others 2, 3 and 4?
- **Is the house part of what "Chapter 1 finished" means?** Now the chapter's ending comes when all four are
  finished. Players who finished the three levels before the house was added keep their ending and are not asked to
  play the house.
- **Is there still a treasure hunt?** The torn map was taken out of Level 2. What are the siblings looking for, and
  how is that shown without words?
- **The siblings' names.** They are "the sister" and "the brother" for now.
- **Should they meet people** (a shopkeeper, a ferryman)? Chapter 1 has only animals.

## 4. Chapter 2, the forest

- Chapter 1 ends with the forest across the river, behind a padlock on the map. The approved Forest Trail scene
  ([game/forest.html](game/forest.html)) is the look. Nothing of Chapter 2 is designed yet.
- What are its new ideas? By the progression rules: one new idea per level, and progress through combinations of
  known tools. Two combination ideas are waiting in [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md) (the boat roof, the balloon on
  the see-saw).
- How many levels, and how long each? The rules say levels grow slowly towards about 15 minutes.
- Does the toss come back, and the pulley and the heavy crate, which Level 3 left out?

## 5. Things saved for later

- **Stickers and a scrapbook.** Collecting was taken out of Chapter 1. When it comes back: what is collecting for,
  where are the stickers kept (a scrapbook?), and how is it shown so that it does not look like a task?
- **The dog and the ball.** Kept in `game/shared/later/`. Which level gets them back? Is the dog only a friend that
  tags along, or does it do something in a puzzle? A game of fetch would need picking up and throwing, which no level
  has taught.
- **The hurdle and the puddle** from the first park: their code is still in the park's `things.js`, not placed.
- **Not built at all:** a calm ride between places (the metro was cut), speech or sounds for the siblings' voices.

## 6. Playing online, and hidden information

- **Online play.** The first idea was two players on their own devices (old decision 3). Chapter 1 is two players on
  one screen. Is online play still wanted later? It would need a server, a way to find each other (room code or
  link), and a way to talk without voice (pings, picture messages).
- **Split information** (decision 5) and **answers that change every game** (decision 22) are not used in Chapter 1.
  With separate screens they could come back for older players: one sees the clue, the other has the lock. Wanted?

## 7. Releasing the game

- **Monetization.** Nothing is decided or built. How is the game paid for: a one-off price, a free first chapter with
  later chapters paid, or something else? Whatever is chosen has to suit a mixed audience that includes children
  (decision 25), which limits advertising and in-game purchases; this needs proper advice before it is built.
- **Where it is released.** It runs in a browser on computers, tablets and phones. Web only, or app stores as well?
- **Which version is public.** The main web address still shows an older version (the house and the first city
  level). The approved Chapter 1 is on a separate preview address. Should the main address show Chapter 1 now?
  (The addresses are in [CHECKPOINTS.md](CHECKPOINTS.md).)
- **Privacy.** The game stores only its progress, on the device. That should stay true if anything is added.
- **Languages.** Nothing needs reading, so nothing needs translating. Keep it that way?

## 8. Housekeeping in the code (no player would notice)

- The code that runs a level exists three times, once per level (`worlds/park/level.js`, `worlds/city/lab.js`,
  `worlds/canal/level.js`). Before Chapter 2, pull the common part into `game/shared/`.
- Level 2's folder and files are still named after the test level it began as (`worlds/city/`, `lab.js`,
  `LAB_PLAN.md`). Rename, or leave?
- In Levels 1 and 2 the picture pushes the sibling who stays behind; in Level 3 it does not (there it had pushed her
  into the water). Make all three the same?
- The music files are large for the web. Make lighter copies?

## Reference: research so far

The full research report is in
[reports/Puzzle design in story platformers.md](reports/Puzzle%20design%20in%20story%20platformers.md); the notes behind it
are in `research_notes/`. Lessons that still hold:
- Different roles let players with different skill levels enjoy playing together.
- Design for exactly two from the start; do not add co-op later.
- Teach one idea at a time, alone, before combining it with anything.
